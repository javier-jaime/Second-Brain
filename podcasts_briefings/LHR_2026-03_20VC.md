The origins of **Simile** are rooted in a 2023 experiment conducted at **Stanford** known as the Smallville project. This simulation featured a town of 25 non-playable characters (NPCs) that exhibited autonomous behaviors, such as planning parties and maintaining routines, by utilizing large language models paired with a specific agentic architecture.

### **The Agentic Workflow**

To move beyond simple classification tasks, the founders developed three core components for their agents:

* Memory: Early models lacked long term recall, leading to repetitive interactions. The team initially solved this by storing experiences in markdown text files, allowing agents to recognize recurring characters and events.  
* Planning: Agents use their memory to organize their days and adhere to routines, ensuring their actions are consistent with their stated personas.  
* Reflection: This function acts as a higher level processing mechanism, similar to a shower thought. Agents periodically review their accumulated memories to formulate higher level ideas about their identities and motivations.

"Gradually they start to realize, oh this particular research topic I'm actually quite invested in it, this might have actually had something to do with my childhood, or my fundamental memory, this actually shapes who they are as a person."

While frontier model providers aim for superrationality, **Simile** focuses on the subjective half of the brain. The objective is to represent human values, preferences, and tastes, including the biases and mistakes that characterize real human behavior. This approach requires a move away from standard web data, which primarily captures what people say rather than what they actually do.

A central thesis for **Simile** is that AI companies must possess a unique and defensible data strategy. The company utilizes a multipronged approach to capture the chasm between human speech and action.

### **Sourcing and Representation**

**Simile** does not seek expert programmers or scientists for its data. Instead, it targets everyday people to ensure simulations are representative of the global population. Data collection begins with deep biographical inquiries, asking participants to recount their life stories, hardships, and pivotal decisions to map the core nature of their personalities.

### **Causal Mechanisms vs. Prediction**

The company posits that prediction alone is of limited value to enterprises. For instance, knowing that sales will decline does not help a company like **Starbucks** unless they understand how to change that outcome.

* Counterfactuals: **Simile** uses randomized control trials and A/B testing to understand how behaviors change under different conditions.  
* Validation: The company uses the real world as its ground truth. By generating thousands of hypotheses daily and comparing them against actual global events, the model experiences a continuous reward loop.

"The world is our ground truth."

Founder assessment focuses on the spike of an individual, their distance traveled, and their vulnerability. As the market shifts toward an agent based economy, **Sequoia Capital** anticipates a new class of trillion dollar companies that masquerade as service businesses, while delivering software like margins through end to end outcome automation.

## **Organizational Culture and Sourcing Dynamics**

The internal culture of **Sequoia Capital** is defined by a rigorous, competitive, and proactive approach to venture capital.

### **The Sports Team Mentality**

The firm characterizes itself not as a family, but as a professional sports team. This model requires that every member, regardless of seniority, contributes to the field. Performance is expected of all individuals, and the firm operates on the principle that they win as a collective unit. This is exemplified by the work ethic of senior partners such as [Doug Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Leone), who maintains a high level of intensity and early morning commitment.

### **Active Sourcing and Persistence**

The partnership rejects the notion that they merely wait for high profile companies to contact them. "Everyone at **Sequoia** is a hunter." Sourcing involves long term relationship building, sometimes spanning years before an investment is realized. A notable example is [Konstantine Buhler](https://www.linkedin.com/in/konstantinebuhler)'s multi-year effort to build a relationship with [Ken Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_C._Griffin) of **Citadel Securities**, which eventually allowed **Sequoia Capital** to become the first outside investor in the firm.

### **Performance Principles**

The firm adheres to the belief that its reputation is entirely dependent on future performance. "We are only as good as our next investment." This creates an environment of humility and preparation, ensuring that partners do not rest on historical successes like **SpaceX** or **Airbnb**.

## **The Investment Committee and Decision-making Frameworks**

**Sequoia Capital** has utilized a consistent Monday Investment Committee (IC) format for five decades, but it is currently experimenting with new methodologies to improve decision quality.

### **Fast vs. Slow Thinking**

The firm is integrating asynchronous contributions into its decision process.

* **Traditional IC:** Focused on fast thinking, where partners discuss a deal sequentially in a live meeting.  
* **Asynchronous Experimentation:** Partners contribute to a shared document after a memo is distributed. This encourages slow thinking and allows the best ideas to surface before a live discussion occurs.

### **Voting and Sponsor Conviction**

The firm uses a 1 to 10 scale for voting on potential investments.

* **Controversy and Conviction:** The most successful investments are often the most controversial, or those where the sponsoring partner possessed the highest conviction.  
* **The Power to Proceed:** A sponsor can still choose to move forward with an investment, even if other partners provide negative feedback, though doing so carries significant internal risk.  
* **Team Support:** Once a decision is made, it becomes a firm-wide commitment. Every partner is expected to use their network and resources to support the founder, regardless of their initial vote.

### **Revisiting Priors**

A critical component of the firm's strategy is mental flexibility. Partners are encouraged to update their priors when environmental conditions change. This was evidenced by the decision to invest in **Anthropic** at a high valuation despite passing on earlier opportunities, recognizing that the exponential growth of AI required a shift in traditional valuation frameworks.

## **Founder Evaluation and Assessment Strategies**

Assessing founders involves identifying unique spikes, and understanding the individual's trajectory.

### **The Thirty Minute Spike**

Partners aim to identify what makes an individual exceptional within the first thirty minutes of a meeting. This requires moving past the transactional pitch to understand the person's history and character.

### **Vulnerability and Truth Seeking**

To get founders to open up, partners often lead with their own personal stories of adversity. This creates a space for the founder to share their genuine narrative. The firm looks for distance traveled, comparing where a founder started in life, to where they are now. A founder who has overcome significant personal or systemic challenges, often demonstrates a steeper trajectory.

### **Reference Checking Techniques**

The firm employs specific questioning during the reference checking process:

* **The Worst Reference:** [Doug Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Leone) utilizes a tactic of asking a founder who their best reference is, followed immediately by who their worst reference would be. The honesty and texture of the response provide clarity on the founder's self awareness and history.  
* **The ELO Framework:** Influenced by [Shaun Maguire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaun_Maguire), the firm uses an Elo style scoring to judge outliers. The premise is that only an outlier can accurately identify another outlier.

### **Evaluating Character and Skill**

The firm utilizes several frameworks to categorize talent:

* **Vectors:** [Pat Grady](https://www.linkedin.com/in/gradypb) views founders as vectors, which are the product of direction (motivation and goals) and magnitude (ambition and resilience).  
* **Outlier Operators vs. Outlier Founders:** [Alfred Lin](https://www.linkedin.com/in/linalfred) warns against confusing a high performing employee at a major firm like **OpenAI** or **DeepMind** with a true founder capable of building a company from zero.  
* **IQ/EQ vs. Judgment/PQ:** Intellectual and emotional horsepower are weighed against judgment (finding solutions in complex systems) and political coefficient (navigating complex environments).
