# **Inside The Startup Building Reusable Rockets**

## **Executive Summary**

**Stoke Space** is developing a fully and rapidly reusable rocket system designed to achieve aircraft-like operational frequency. While current industry leaders have mastered the reuse of first stage boosters, **Stoke Space** focuses on the second stage capsule, which is historically discarded due to the extreme thermal and mechanical stresses of atmospheric reentry. By utilizing a novel heat shield cooled by liquid hydrogen and maintaining total vertical integration of manufacturing, the company aims to drastically lower launch costs and increase flight availability. To date, the company has raised $90 million and is preparing for its first orbital launch.

## **The Mission of Stoke Space**

The central objective of **Stoke Space** is to solve the most difficult problem in rocketry: the rapid reuse of the entire vehicle. Current space flight is limited by the fact that second stages, which are multimillion dollar machines, are thrown away after every mission.

* **Market Current State:** There are approximately 150 commercial launches annually, which is a record but insufficient for a ubiquitous space economy.  
* **Infrastructure Barriers:** Most current launch availability is consumed by **Starlink**, leaving other customers with high costs and limited access.  
* **The App Store Analogy:** Founders compare the potential of rapid reusability to the iPhone app store, suggesting that reliable and frequent access will lead to "absolutely crazy ideas" for space applications that are currently inconceivable.

## **Technical Architecture: Nova and Andromeda**

The **Stoke Space** launch system consists of a two stage rocket designated as Nova, with a specialized second stage capsule named Andromeda.

| Component | Function and Features |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Nova (First Stage)** | Operates similarly to existing reusable boosters, returning to the launch site or landing downrange. It features one of the highest performing, fuel efficient engines ever produced to ensure long life and reusability. |
| **Andromeda (Second Stage)** | Designed to survive reentry from orbit at 17,000 mph. It uses a custom heat shield and 24 small thrusters to manage the descent and land at a precise angle. |

### **Innovations in Reentry Technology**

The second stage must survive temperatures exceeding 2700° F. **Stoke Space** employs a proprietary heat exchanger system that uses cold liquid hydrogen flowing through the heat shield to absorb extreme thermal energy. This approach allows the capsule to remain intact and ready for rapid turnaround rather than breaking down during its descent.

## **Operational Philosophy and Vertical Integration**

**Stoke Space** emphasizes speed of iteration as the primary driver of technical success. This is achieved through a philosophy of building everything in-house.

* **Development Speed:** By maintaining an internal machine shop and additive manufacturing capabilities, the company has reduced the iteration cycle for parts from one month to approximately two days.  
* **Risk Management:** The company maintains a "next one ready" policy on the factory floor. If a test fails, a replacement engine or component is immediately available to incorporate lessons learned without delaying the schedule.  
* **Facility Capacity:** The current 168,000 square foot factory in Kent, Washington, is designed to produce seven vehicles per year.

### **The Role of Software**

The founders identified software as a critical component for scaling from a garage startup to a flight ready organization. They developed a custom internal tool called Boltline to manage operations.

* **Automation:** Boltline automates the tracking of part service life and maintenance schedules.  
* **Data Abstraction:** The software allows factory workers to log data seamlessly, which is then sliced and analyzed for different engineering and management functions.

## **Organizational History and Growth**

The company was founded in September 2019 by two former **Blue Origin** propulsion engineers who identified a lack of focus in the industry regarding full second stage reusability.

* **Early Prototyping:** Initial rocket engine testing was conducted in a shipping container located in a residential backyard. The founders built a pressure fed gaseous hydrogen and liquid oxygen thruster to demonstrate hardware capability to early investors.  
* **Y Combinator (YC):** The founders joined **YC** to overcome a lack of fundraising connections and to learn how to navigate the venture capital environment, particularly as a hardware company in a market often geared toward software.  
* **Funding:** The company has raised $90 million to date, maintaining high capital efficiency compared to industry peers.

### **Key Milestones**

* **2019:** Founding of **Stoke Space** and initial backyard prototype development.  
* **2022:** Completion of the first full engine test.  
* **2024:** Relocation to the 168,000 square foot headquarters and manufacturing facility.  
* **Future:** Construction is currently underway at Complex 14 in Cape Canaveral, Florida, for a dedicated launch site. An orbital launch is planned for late 2026\.

# **Ankit Gupta on Early User Acquisition and Product Evolution**

## **Executive Summary**

The transition from a new product to a market success depends on the ability to identify and engage a specific subset of the population known as early adopters. For most startups, the initial challenge is not a lack of persuasion but a search problem. The objective is to locate individuals with a burning issue or those who naturally enjoy testing new technology. Startups must shift their focus from building a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) to a Minimum Evolvable Product (MEP), which is a version designed to survive contact with users and adapt based on their feedback. By charging for the product immediately, using targeted outreach, and conducting rapid experiments, founders can gather the sharp feedback necessary to steer the product through its evolutionary stages. The long term trajectory of a product is path dependent, meaning the preferences of early users will fundamentally dictate the features and identity of the final mature version.

## **Redefining the Early User Acquisition Process**

Acquiring the first ten users is a significant hurdle, because the vast majority of people are not early adopters, and do not wish to be a company’s first paying customer. Successfully finding these initial users requires a shift in mindset.

* **The Search Problem:** Finding the first users is less about convincing disinterested parties and more about locating those who are already looking for a solution.  
* **Profiles of Early Adopters:**  
  * **Enthusiasts:** Individuals who enjoy the process of trying new products and bringing them into their professional environments.  
  * **Desperate Solvers:** Users with a burning issue who are willing to take a chance on an unproven product, if it promises to solve their specific problem.

"Finding your first users is more of a search problem, than a persuasion problem."

## **Tactical Execution for Startups**

The strategies used to find a mass market, are often ineffective for finding the first few users. Startups must employ high touch, targeted methods.

### **Revenue as a Feedback Mechanism**

Charging money from the outset is recommended not for the sake of revenue, but to ensure the quality of user feedback. Paying customers are more invested and provide more critical, actionable insights than those using a free version.

"Paying customers give sharper feedback than free users ever will."

Early adopters are generally not price sensitive, if their problem is severe enough.

### **Outreach and Launch Strategy**

Startups are encouraged to launch early to maximize the surface area for discovery. Outreach should be personal and highly specific.

* **Methods:** Targeted cold emails or direct personal interactions, are more effective than broad advertising like billboards.  
* **Anthropological Approach:** Founders should study their early users with the intensity of an anthropologist, seeking to understand why these individuals made the unusual choice to trust an unproven startup.

### **Rapid Experimentation and Churn**

Startups have a unique advantage over large corporations because their failures are not public.

"You're fighting irrelevance, not headlines."

* **Constant Iteration:** Pricing, landing pages, and features should all be tested simultaneously.  
* **Managing Churn:** While founders should strive to make users love the product, they should not fear losing a single user, as many more potential customers exist who have not yet heard of the product.

## **The Economic Reality of Consumer vs. Corporate Markets**

The financial context of the target user significantly impacts the viability of a startup, particularly in the era of Artificial Intelligence.

| Market Segment | Characteristics |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Personal Software Spend** | Low average budget, approximately $150 per month. New apps must compete for a small remaining portion of this budget. |
| **Corporate Software Spend** | Significantly higher budget. A single tool on a corporate card can cost more than an individual's entire monthly personal software spend. |
| **AI Considerations** | Advertising revenue often fails to cover the high infrastructure costs of AI, making subscriptions necessary. |

Due to these economic pressures, many founders, including those at **Y Combinator**, often choose to target prosumers, businesses, or high value professionals like doctors who have high advertising value.

## **The Phylogenetic Tree of Product Evolution**

A startup can be viewed through the lens of a phylogenetic tree, where the product evolves from a simple organism into a complex one.

* **The Amoeba Stage:** The initial product version is an amoeba, possessing only the basic functions required to survive external market pressures.  
  "The earliest version of your product only needs to do one thing, survive contact with a tiny group of people who might actually try it."  
* **Path Dependency:** The eventual form of the product, such as a dog or a human in the evolutionary analogy, is determined by the specific pressures exerted by early users.

## **Case Study: Path Dependency in Tesla Development**

The development of **Tesla** illustrates how early adopters influence the final form of a mass market product.

* **The Amoeba (Roadster):** The **Tesla** Roadster was an impractical, expensive vehicle that lacked public charging infrastructure and interior space.  
* **Early Adopter Influence:** The people willing to buy the Roadster valued technology and acceleration over traditional automotive virtues, like comfort and suspension.  
* **The Mass Market Result:** Because early users prioritized performance, the mass market Model Y features a 0 to 60 time comparable to a **Lamborghini**, but has suspension and comfort levels that may be inferior to a **Toyota**.  
* **Conclusion:** If the original buyers had demanded a slow, plush vehicle, the current **Tesla** lineup would look entirely different. The evolution of the product is inextricably linked to the preferences of those who first supported it.

# **Diffusion in Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence**

## **Executive Summary**

Diffusion has emerged as a fundamental Machine Learning framework capable of learning data distributions across nearly any domain, provided sufficient data is available. Originally gaining prominence through image generation, the technology has expanded into protein folding, robotics, weather forecasting, and drug discovery. The core advantage of diffusion lies in its ability to map between high dimensional spaces, even when operating in low data regimes. While traditional large language models process information as an iterative, token by token sequence, diffusion leverages randomness and conceptual thinking, attributes that align more closely with biological intelligence. Recent mathematical innovations, particularly flow matching, have significantly simplified the implementation of these models, reducing the core logic to approximately ten lines of code. For founders and researchers, diffusion represents a dominant trend that is currently overtaking most areas of Artificial Intelligence, with autoregressive models and game playing algorithms remaining the final holdouts of non diffusion state of the art performance.

## **Fundamental Principles of Diffusion**

Diffusion is a Machine Learning framework designed to learn the probability distribution of data. Its primary strength is the mapping of high dimensions to high dimensions, which is particularly effective when training samples are limited relative to the dimensionality of the space.

### **The Noising and Denoising Process**

The mechanism operates through a two part procedure:

* Forward Process: A data sample, such as an image, is iteratively hit with noise until its original structure is destroyed, and it becomes random static.  
* Reverse Process: The model is trained to reverse this noising procedure. It learns to take noised up images, and identify intermediate representations to return to the original data distribution.

### **The Noise Schedule**

The most difficult component of diffusion to master is the noise schedule, which dictates how error is introduced.

* Instability of Linear Interpolation: Adding noise linearly is unstable because the relative amount of error added is small at the start, and too large at the end.  
* Beta Schedules: Effective models use a beta schedule, often appearing as a sigmoid curve, where the rate of diffusion ensures a relatively constant amount of error, is introduced at every time step.  
* Alpha and Alpha Bar: These terms represent the weights used to determine how much original data is retained, versus how much noise is added during the training process.

## **Evolution and Mathematical Innovation**

The field has progressed from complex probabilistic models, to streamlined mathematical architectures. This evolution is characterized by a process of hill climbing on the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) metric, a measure used to evaluate the quality of generated data.

### **Shifts in Training Objectives**

Researchers have found that different loss functions impact how easily a model learns:

* Predicting Raw Data: Early models attempted to predict the original data directly, which proved difficult.  
* Predicting Error or Velocity: It is mathematically simpler and more effective for the model, to predict the error added to a sample, or the velocity of the change between states.  
* Flow Matching: Developed at **Meta**, this approach replaces circuitous paths from noise to data, with a global velocity.  
  "Instead and like intuitively, it's like okay we're doing the circuitous path, but surely there's a shorter path between those two and so, that's what makes flow matching so cool to me, at least is that they said, forget all of that intermediary results, there is a velocity, a global velocity between the noise and the data, and it's just this direction, it's just this straight line."

### **Implementation Simplicity**

Despite the sophistication of the results, the fundamental math of modern diffusion is concise. A powerful Machine Learning procedure can be written in roughly ten to fifteen lines of code, regardless of whether the data is images, weather patterns, or proteins. The complexity of modern AI arises from scaling these simple mathematical foundations, to billions of parameters and massive data centers, rather than from the underlying logic.

## **Applications Across Industries**

Diffusion is being deployed across a wide variety of high impact fields, beyond simple image and video generation.

| Domain | Application | Key Entity / Model |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Life Sciences | Protein folding and small molecule binding | **DeepMind** AlphaFold, DiffDock |
| Robotics | Developing robotic policies and autonomous actions | Diffusion Policy |
| Meteorology | Highly accurate weather forecasting | GenCast |
| Media | Image and video generation | Midjourney, Sora, Flux, Stable Diffusion |
| Software | Code generation and text | Diffusion LLMs |

"Diffusion has eaten all of AI, except two AR LLMs still are outperforming, and gameplay and things like AlphaGo."

Aside from these areas, diffusion has become the standard for state of the art performance.

## **Diffusion and General Intelligence**

Diffusion models offer a different perspective on the path toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) compared to monolithic transformer stacks used in large language models (LLMs).

### **The Squint Test**

The squint test suggests that while Artificial Intelligence does not need to perfectly mimic biology, it should share core functional similarities with the only known proof of intelligence: the brain.

* Randomness: Biology leverages randomness. Diffusion models utilize randomness to learn about data distributions.  
* Conceptual Thinking: Unlike LLMs that produce one token at a time without the ability to go backward, diffusion involves thinking in concepts and recursively improving an output.  
* Action Space: LLMs are often bottlenecked by an action space that only allows for one token at a time, whereas diffusion can emit and refine large chunks of thought or data simultaneously.

## **Strategic Outlook for Founders and Researchers**

The rapid improvement of diffusion models over the last five years suggests that performance gains are highly tractable through scale, data, and time.

### **Guidance for Technical Leaders**

* Incorporate Diffusion in Training: Machine learning teams should evaluate diffusion procedures as a fundamental piece of their training loops, regardless of their specific application.  
* Update Priors on Capability: The transition from early models like Midjourney to modern systems like Sora represents a thousand fold improvement. Founders should anticipate similar trajectories in robotics, DNA analysis, and metabolomics.  
* Address Limitations: Current diffusion models are constrained by the number of steps used during training. If a model is trained for 100 steps, it generally cannot be called for more steps at test time to increase resolution without specific distillation techniques.

"I think it's going to redefine the entire economy."

This sentiment reflects the view that the core mathematical simplicity of diffusion, combined with its broad applicability and scalability, positions it as the primary engine for the next wave of AI development.

# **Startup Website Design and Conversion Strategies**

## **Executive Summary**

The analysis of several startup websites, conducted by the CEO of **Framer**, reveals that high conversion rates are driven by product transparency, reduced user friction, and purposeful animation. A recurring failure across the reviewed platforms is the reliance on generic illustrations and marketing language rather than showing the actual user interface or functionality. The most critical takeaways include the necessity of establishing a product's value within the first ten seconds, the risk of high commitment calls to action, like booking a demo before providing value, and the importance of meticulous attention to detail in typography and spacing, to establish brand trust.

## **Core Strategic Themes in Website Conversion**

### **The Requirement for Product Transparency**

A central theme identified is the detrimental effect of hiding the actual product behind abstract graphics or high level value propositions. Users require an anchor to understand what they are using and why.

* **Product Visuals:** Websites should prioritize screenshots or short video walkthroughs over generic icons. This provides evidence that the product is real and functional.  
* **Aha Moments:** The goal of a landing page should be to help a visitor experience the product's value, as quickly as possible.  
  "I'm not going to book a demo, without getting us just a little bit of a taste of what the product looks like, and how it works."  
* **Contextual Communication:** Graphics and animations must not only look aesthetically pleasing but must also communicate specific information about the product's workflow.

### **Managing User Friction and Conversion Paths**

The review highlights a significant disconnect between what startups want (booked demos and signups), and what users are willing to give upon their first visit.

* **Call to Action (CTA) Gravity:** Buttons like "Book a demo" are often too dramatic for a first time visitor.  
  "You probably first need to convince me what this is and what it's about, before I will do something as dramatic as booking a demo, which is taking my time, and now I need to be on a call with a person for 20 minutes."  
* **Frictionless Entry:** For tools competing with free alternatives, such as **Google** Slides, making the entry point frictionless is vital. Roadblocks like mandatory signups before trying a core feature, can lead to high drop-off rates.  
* **The 10 Second Rule:** Users typically decide if a product is for them within five to ten seconds.  
  "You really have to make it obvious if 5 to 10 seconds, that's that, and I think 10 seconds is a long time for people to try and figure out if this is for them."

### **The Strategic Use of Animation and Aesthetics**

Animation can enhance a site when used to draw attention to functionality, but it often becomes a distraction.

* **Tasteful Execution:** Animation should make things overly obvious, or guide the user's eye to a specific action.  
  "Animation, I feel in general, is like pretty challenging to do well, because it can be distracting very fast."  
* **Technical Details:** Small errors, such as inconsistent spacing, typos, or capitalization in quotes, subconsciously signal a lack of detail oriented leadership.  
* **Ticker Effects:** Speed should be managed carefully, for example, logo tickers should slow down on hover, to allow users to interact or find context.

## **Detailed Review of Startup Websites**

### **Lumari**

**Lumari** is an AI platform for procurement and supply chain operations. The review identified several areas where the site's complexity hinders clarity.

* **Visual Overload:** The site features excessive movement and icons that distract from the core message. The orange on orange color scheme causes the primary CTA buttons to get lost.  
* **Abstract Graphics:** The hero animation uses vague metaphors, such as envelopes, which fail to explain the product to a new visitor.  
* **Navigation Issues:** Important information is hidden behind tabs. A recommended alternative is to connect tab switching to scrolling behavior to lead the user through the content.  
* **Key Feedback:** The highest leverage improvement would be replacing abstract illustrations with product screenshots or a video walkthrough to make the platform feel real.

### **Alai**

**Alai** focuses on creating high quality presentations using AI, positioning itself as an alternative to **Gamma**.

* **Competitive Positioning:** The site successfully uses quotes to counterposition against **Gamma**, though it lacks a clear subheadline to explain exactly what the tool is upfront.  
* **Quality Standards:** Claiming high quality is risky, as standards vary by user. The current output feels somewhat templated, suggesting that the brand might benefit more from a focus on speed and ease of use.  
* **The Signup Barrier:** The site misses an opportunity by forcing a login immediately. A more effective strategy would be a demo mode or a prompt bar on the front page, similar to the functionality of **Google** Slides.

### **Juicebox**

**Juicebox** is an AI recruiting platform recently funded by **Sequoia**. It utilizes a brutalist, modern aesthetic.

* **Brand Aesthetic:** The site feels fresh and AI forward, yet there is a disconnect between the brutalist, black and white style and the human-centric nature of recruiting. The site lacks human faces or avatars.  
* **Messaging:** The headline "Win the talent war" is strong and attention grabbing, though it contrasts with the playful name **Juicebox**.  
* **Interactive Friction:** While it allows users to start a search prompt, it quickly hits a login roadblock, which prevents users from seeing the immediate value of the search results.

### **Leaping AI**

**Leaping AI** provides voice AI agents for enterprise call centers.

* **Interactive Demo:** The site features a successful, low friction interactive demo where users can speak with an AI agent directly in the browser.  
* **Animation Excellence:** The pulsing background and immersive scrolling interactions are cited as examples of high quality animation that adds value without being distracting.  
* **Enterprise Alignment:** Despite clear text-based positioning for large scale enterprises, the visual choices (a single person on an iPhone) feel more like a consumer product. To align with enterprise needs, visuals should depict distributed systems or larger scale operations.

### **The Hog**

**The Hog** offers "Growth as a Service" (GaaS), aiming to automate sales and marketing strategies.

* **Professionalism Concerns:** The site suffers from significant detail-oriented issues, including inconsistent double spacing between words and a logo that resembles an emoji.  
* **Credibility Gaps:** Customer quotes use unusual capitalization (capitalizing every word), which suggests they may not be authentic.  
  "The chances that both of these people actually write by capitalizing the first word of every sentence, I don't believe, and therefore I don't believe these are real quotes as a result."  
* **Layout Issues:** The landing page lacks a maximum width, making the text difficult to read. The review suggests a complete simplification, focusing only on elements that help the user understand the core value proposition.

# **The Y Combinator Website Redesign**

## **Executive Summary**

The recent redesign of the **Y Combinator** website represents a fundamental shift from a utilitarian, sales oriented interface to a storytelling driven platform designed to inspire founders. The project sought to move away from the aesthetic of a standard business to business software template, which previously emphasized corporate logos and generic success metrics. Instead, the new design prioritizes the human element, specifically the trajectory of founders from humble beginnings to the leadership of generational companies. Key innovations in the redesign include the integration of Artificial Intelligence for both coding and content creation, the use of founder-led narratives instead of marketing copy, and a commitment to a minimalist aesthetic that removes traditional call to action distractions. The process was notable for its departure from static design tools in favor of real-time prototyping using Opus 4.5 and AI driven animation tools developed by **Y Combinator** startups.

## **Critiques of the Legacy Design**

The design team identified several deficiencies in the previous version of the website, which had been in place for approximately five years.

* **Utilitarian Aesthetic:** The previous site was described as feeling like a B2B SaaS template, featuring a standard hero section with a call to action, basic statistics, and a grid of corporate logos.  
* **Logo-Centricity:** While the site displayed the logos of successful companies, it failed to highlight the founders behind those entities.  
* **Abstract Messaging:** The headlines were identified as generic and failed to quantify the actual impact of the program. "The results speak for themselves" was cited as a headline that failed to illustrate specific outcomes or the scale of success.  
* **Negative Framing:** The section regarding founder interests focused primarily on what the organization does not do, such as taking board seats or requiring decks, rather than presenting a positive, affirmative vision.

## **Philosophical and Narrative Shift**

The core mission of the redesign was to make prospective founders dream ,rather than simply selling them a program. This was achieved by rooting the site in the historical values of **Y Combinator**.

* **The Concept of Formidability:** The design team drew inspiration from the essays of [Paul Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Graham_\(programmer\)), specifically the word formidable, which [Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Graham_\(programmer\)) and [Jessica Livingston](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jessicalivingston1) used to describe the extraordinary founders they funded in the early days.  
* **Founder Transformation:** A central section of the new site illustrates the before and after states of successful founders. By showing current industry leaders alongside photos from their early, humble days in the program, the site aims to help current builders see themselves in those stories.  
* **Authentic Testimony:** Rather than using internal marketing copy to describe the experience, the team interviewed founders from recent batches.  
  "We gathered these small sound bites together and put them side by side, so that it feels like continuous text that you can read, but they are actually all quotes coming from various founders from different batches."  
* **Humanizing Partners:** To emphasize that all partners were once founders themselves, the site includes interactive cards showing partners when they were participants in the program.

## **Aesthetic and Functional Design Choices**

The visual direction of the site was intentionally minimal to ensure that the storytelling remained the focal point.

* **Breathable Layout:** The design team avoided hard dividers and borders to create an airy feel where content appears to float on the page.  
* **Historical Continuity:** Despite the visual overhaul, the team retained the original background color and the core descriptive copy written by [Paul Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Graham_\(programmer\)) over a decade ago.  
  "The only thing that we tweaked is some details to make it up to date, like for example, now we fund companies four times a year, and it's in San Francisco, no longer in Mountain View, but other than that the copy is the exact same, as what it was on the very original YC website 15 years ago."  
* **Removal of Top Level CTAs:** The team chose not to include a prominent apply button in the hero section. The rationale was that **Y Combinator** is not a standard software product looking for a minor conversion rate increase, but a brand seeking to inspire deep engagement.  
* **Inclusive Messaging:** The final section of the site explicitly states that it is never too early to apply, targeting founders who may feel they lack sufficient revenue or a fully formed idea.

## **Technical Implementation and AI Integration**

The methodology used to build the site was as significant as the design itself, relying heavily on emerging technology and interactive prototyping.

### **AI Assisted Development**

The team moved quickly from **Figma** mood boards into a live code repository, using Opus 4.5 within the **Cursor** environment to explore directions. This allowed the team to treat the AI as a coworker that could cook up creative ways to display information.

### **Interactive Animation**

The site utilizes interactivity and animation not for decoration, but to communicate the core message of transformation.

* **Animated Portraits:** To make the experience feel alive, the team used AI to animate photos of recognizable faces in the startup ecosystem.  
* **Tool Selection:** After testing various tools that produced unrecognizable results, the team utilized **Awen**, a startup from a recent **Y Combinator** batch, to generate authentic video animations.

### **Table: Comparison of Design Eras**

| Feature | Legacy Design | New Design |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Primary Focus** | Utilitarian/Program Stats | Storytelling/Founder Inspiration |
| **Visual Elements** | Corporate Logos | Founder Portraits and Transformations |
| **Copy Origin** | Marketing focused | Historical [PG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Graham_\(programmer\)) Essays and Founder Quotes |
| **Design Process** | Static frames in **Figma** | AI assisted live prototyping |
| **Navigation** | Standard B2B SAS Layout | Minimalist, Floating Content |
| **Call to Action** | Prominent Apply buttons | Narrative driven leading to final CTA |

## **Conclusion**

The redesign reflects a broader shift in web and product design, away from static images and text. The creators anticipate an evolution toward using interactivity and animation, as primary communication tools. By focusing on high level storytelling and leveraging AI to handle basic coding tasks, designers can dedicate more bandwidth to how a site communicates its essential message.

"One thing that we wanted to show on industry design, is that YC is also fun and playful, going through YC is fun, and we don't take ourselves too seriously and so, using all these pictures of like the early days, of part of partners who were going through YC, or some of the top founders, it makes it playful."

# **AI Coding Agents: Evolution, Architecture, and the Manager Mode**

## **Executive Summary**

The landscape of software development is undergoing a fundamental transformation driven by the emergence of high capability AI coding agents like **Anthropic**’s Claude Code and **OpenAI**’s Codex. This shift is characterized by a move away from traditional Integrated Development Environments (IDEs) toward Command Line Interface (CLI) tools that prioritize speed, composability, and managerial oversight. Key insights from industry experts suggest that the most significant technical challenge remains context management, navigating the dumb zone where models lose coherence as context windows fill.

As developers transition into a manager mode, the value of senior level architectural taste and fundamental systems knowledge increases. Simultaneously, the barriers to entry for complex software creation are falling, potentially leading to a future of smaller, more prolific teams and highly customized, agent maintained software stacks.

## **The Evolution of Coding Interfaces**

A surprising retro future trend has emerged where the CLI is outperforming the IDE as the preferred environment for AI agents.

* **Composability and Speed:** The CLI provides the purest form for composable, atomic integrations. Tools like Claude Code allow developers to fly through code with status updates and progress indicators that feel faster than navigating complex file trees in an IDE.  
* **Context Discovery:** Unlike IDEs, which are built for human exploration of files, CLIs allow agents more freedom to traverse the file system programmatically.  
* **Environmental Access:** CLI based agents can easily access local development databases and production environments, enabling them to debug deep, nested issues (e.g., five levels deep in delayed jobs) and write immediate regression tests.  
* **The Bionic Knee Analogy:** For experienced developers who moved into management, these agents act as a total knee replacement, allowing them to return to high speed coding with the productivity of five people.

## **Architectural Deep Dive**

# **Artificial General Intelligence and the Future of Symbolic Learning**

## **Executive Summary**

The current trajectory of Artificial Intelligence, dominated by Large Language Models (LLMs) and deep learning, is approaching a pivot point where scaling alone will no longer suffice to achieve Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). [François Chollet](https://www.linkedin.com/in/fchollet), founder of the ARC-AGI benchmark and the research lab **Ndea**, posits that AGI will likely be achieved by 2030\. However, this milestone will not be reached through the existing parametric, gradient descent based stack, but through a new paradigm of symbolic program synthesis.

Key takeaways include:

* **Intelligence as Efficiency:** AGI should be defined as human-level skill acquisition efficiency on new tasks, rather than the mere automation of economically valuable work.  
* **The ARC-AGI Progression:** The benchmark has evolved from measuring reasoning (V1) and verified task harnessing (V2) to measuring agentic intelligence (V3), which requires active exploration and goal setting in unknown environments.  
* **Optimality over Scale:** Future AI will trend toward optimality, characterized by concise symbolic models that require significantly less data and compute, than current deep learning methods.  
* **Human-Centric Development:** Success in the AI era depends on leveraging these tools for empowerment, focusing on usability in software design, and removing human bottlenecks from the improvement loop.

## **Redefining AGI: Skill Acquisition vs. Automation**

A critical distinction exists between the automation of tasks and the possession of general intelligence. While the industry often defines AGI as a system capable of automating most economically valuable tasks, this definition focuses on output rather than the underlying cognitive process.

* **Human-Level Efficiency:** True general intelligence is the ability to approach an entirely new domain and become competent with the same efficiency as a human, using minimal training data and compute.  
* **The Role of Verifiable Rewards:** Current technology, specifically the LLM stack, can already automate domains where solutions are formally verifiable, such as computer code and mathematics. These domains provide a reliable reward signal that allows models to improve through trial and error.  
* **The Intelligence-Knowledge Trade-off:** There is a constant trade-off between fluid intelligence and prior knowledge. Coding agents today are highly competent not because they are smarter in terms of fluid intelligence, but because they are better trained through post-training environments and execution models.

## **Limitations of the Deep Learning Paradigm**

The current AI industry is heavily invested in the LLM stack, which utilizes parametric curves and gradient descent. While productive, this approach is viewed by [François Chollet](https://www.linkedin.com/in/fchollet) as fundamentally inefficient.

* **The Failure of Gradient Descent:** Research at **Google** Brain indicated that while deep learning models could theoretically represent reasoning algorithms, gradient descent often fails to find them. Instead, the process results in overfit pattern matching rather than generalizable programs.  
* **Data Inefficiency:** LLMs require vast amounts of data, essentially reading the whole internet, which contrasts sharply with human learning.  
* **The Harness Problem:** Current high scores on benchmarks like ARC-AGI-2 are often achieved through human engineered harnesses that provide the model with solution strategies. This reliance on human engineering is a sign that the models themselves lack the intelligence to figure out problems autonomously.

## **The Ndea Approach: Symbolic Program Synthesis**

[François Chollet](https://www.linkedin.com/in/fchollet) and his lab, **Ndea**, are exploring an alternative to deep learning called symbolic program synthesis. This approach seeks to rebuild the Machine Learning stack from the foundation up.

* **Symbolic Descent:** This method replaces parametric curves with the simplest possible symbolic models to explain data. It utilizes a process termed symbolic descent, which is the symbolic space equivalent of gradient descent.  
* **Minimum Description Length:** The approach follows the principle that the shortest model of data, is the one most likely to generalize.  
* **Deep Learning as Guidance:** Rather than being the core engine, deep learning is used as a guidance mechanism to explore the vast combinatorial search space of symbolic programs, similar to the architecture of **OpenAI**'s AlphaZero. (Correction: **Google DeepMind** developed AlphaZero)  
* **Optimality:** Symbolic models are concise, run efficiently at inference time, and generalize better because they are closer to the platonic ideal of the problem.

## **The Evolution of the ARC-AGI Benchmark**

The ARC-AGI benchmark serves as a barometer for the industry's progress toward fluid intelligence. It has progressed through three distinct versions to counter the tendency of researchers to brute force solutions.

### **ARC-AGI Version Comparison**

| Version | Focus | Core Measurement |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

# **Recursion as the Next Scaling Law in Artificial Intelligence**

## **Executive Summary**

The prevailing trend in Artificial Intelligence research is shifting from a reliance on increasing model parameters to a focus on recursion at inference time. Research conducted in 2025 through papers on Hierarchical Reasoning Models (HRM) and Tiny Recursive Models (TRM) suggests that recursive architectures can significantly improve reasoning performance without the massive computational overhead of increasing model size.

Current large language models (LLMs) are limited by their feedforward nature, which prevents them from solving incompressible problems, such as Sudoku or complex sorting, in a single forward pass. Recursive models overcome these limitations by using hidden states as a form of internal memory, similar to a Turing machine tape. The findings demonstrate that a 7 million parameter recursive model can outperform models with hundreds of billions of parameters on specific logic-heavy tasks like the ARC Prize. The integration of high dimensional latent space reasoning with the broad representation capabilities of large models represents the next frontier in AI development.

## **The Limitations of Current Large Language Models**

LLMs based on the transformer architecture primarily operate through a one-shot feedforward process during training. While they appear to reason, their performance is bounded by the number of layers in the network and the data they are trained on.

### **Incompressible Problems and Theoretical Bounds**

Certain tasks are considered incompressible, meaning they cannot be solved faster than a specific theoretical lower bound.

* **Sorting Algorithms:** Comparison sorting has a lower bound $\Omega (n\log n)$ steps. A transformer with fewer layers than the required steps cannot complete the sort in a single forward pass.  
* **Logical Puzzles:** Problems like Sudoku and mazes require iterative steps, where each decision is conditioned on previous ones.  
  "it is actually impossible for the model to map from unsorted list to sorted lists, in a one-shot basis"  
* **Computational Depth:** LLMs lack inherent computational depth, they effectively run out of layers to perform necessary comparisons for complex algorithms.

### **Hacks to Overcome One-Shot Limits**

Researchers currently use two primary methods to force reasoning in LLMs, though both have limitations:

* **Chain of Thought (CoT):** This is recursion in the discrete token space. The model outputs its reasoning, reads it, and then continues. It is limited by human labeled traces and cannot easily discover new algorithms from first principles.  
* **Tool Use:** Models can be trained to call external functions, such as a Python sort function. This relies on preexisting knowledge and does not represent inherent model reasoning.

## **The Evolution of Recursive Architectures**

The move toward recursion is a return to concepts found in Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) networks, which were popular until approximately 2016\.

### **Challenges of Historical RNNs**

* **Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT):** To update weights, the model must be unrolled. As steps increase, gradients can vanish or explode, leading to noisy updates.  
* **Memory vs. Compute:** RNNs require storing activations at every step for backpropagation, which is computationally expensive and memory intensive.  
  "if this were happening in your brain, you would need like a million copies of your brain at every single activation, so that I can backprop through it"

### **Advantages of Latent Space Reasoning**

Unlike CoT, which operates in discrete token space, recursive models operate in a continuous, high dimensional latent space. This space is more expressive and allows for complex internal representations that do not need to be snapped back to discrete tokens until the final output.

## **Hierarchical Reasoning Models (HRM)**

HRM represents a breakthrough in using bioinspired hierarchy to solve logical tasks without pre-training.

### **Architectural Structure**

HRM uses a 27 million parameter architecture trained on a very small dataset, such as 1,000 tasks from the ARC Prize. It utilizes three levels of recursion:

1. **Low-Level Module (LNet):** Operates at a high frequency for low-level features.  
2. **High-Level Module (HNet):** Operates at a lower frequency for higher-level hierarchy.  
3. **Outer Refinement Loop:** A third loop that repeatedly runs the refinement process.

### **Fixed-Point Iteration and DEQ**

HRM avoids the BPTT problem by using a method inspired by deep equilibrium models (DEQs). It performs fixed point iteration, where the model is passed a batch multiple times (e.g., 16 times) to reduce residuals. It uses stop gradients to prevent the need for full unrolling, treating different memory states as a mini-batch.

## **Tiny Recursive Models (TRM)**

TRM further refines the concepts introduced in HRM, simplifying the architecture while increasing performance.

### **Key Refinements and Simplifications**

* **Weight Sharing:** Instead of separate networks for low and high levels, TRM uses a single network (NET) but maintains distinct variable scopes for low-level (ZL) and high-level (ZH) features.  
* **Model Size Reduction:** TRM reduces the parameter count from 27 million to 7 million.  
* **Performance:** Despite its smaller size, TRM improved performance on the ARC Prize 1 from 70 percent to 87 percent.  
* **Latent Recursion:** TRM backpropagates through one full latent recursion step, which was found to be more effective than the HRM approach.

### **Comparison of Recursive Models**

| Feature | Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) | Tiny Recursive Model (TRM) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Parameter Count** | 27 Million | 7 Million |
| **Core Architecture** | Separate LNet and HNet (4 layers each) | Weight shared single NET (1 layer) |
| **Recursion Levels** | Three nested loops | Simplified latent recursion |
| **Training Method** | Fixed-point iteration / Stop gradient | Truncated BPTT (T=1) |
| **Primary Task Success** | State of the art on ARC Prize 1 (70%) | Superior ARC Prize 1 performance (87%) |

## **Theoretical and Biological Foundations**

The shift toward recursion is supported by both biological inspiration and automata theory.

### **Bioplausibility**

The human brain operates with different waves and frequencies across various regions. While machine learning often veers away from strict biological accuracy to favor GPU efficiency, the concept of hierarchical processing remains a powerful launching point for research.

### **Automata Theory**

Recursive models function similarly to a Turing machine with an external memory cache. By training a model to use a hidden state or carry as a memory bank, it can discover algorithms like merge sort or Sudoku solutions without being explicitly shown the steps via human traces.

"the right answer is to like, take the amazingness here, and take the amazingness here"

## **Future Research Directions**

The synthesis of recursion and massive scaling is expected to define the next era of AI research.

* **Combining Recursion with Large Scale:** Current recursive models are task specific. The goal is to apply these recursive reasoning techniques inside the high quality latent spaces generated by massive general-purpose models like those from **Google**.  
* **Variable Compute Depth:** Future models may adjust their compute depth based on the difficulty of the problem, using more recursion for complex tasks and less for simple ones.  
* **Discovery of New Knowledge:** Because recursive models can discover strategies without teacher forcing, they have the potential to surpass the bounds of human knowledge, fulfilling the Einstein test of rebuilding complex physics, or logic from first principles.  
* **Architectural Optimization:** Independent research indicates that the outer refinement loop is the primary driver of success, suggesting that future architectures can be simplified further while maintaining scaling benefits.  
  "If you can make the model really big and you have lots of recursion, and we do something else other than backprop time, then we can get exact all the benefits of this, and all the benefits of the giant LLMs, and then you can get some crazy stuff."

# **The Rise of the Self Improving AI Native Company**

## **Executive Summary**

The traditional corporate structure, modeled after the hierarchical Roman Legion, is being rendered obsolete by Artificial Intelligence. Rather than viewing AI as a simple productivity tool or a bolt-on engine for existing workflows, organizations must be reimagined as sets of recursive, self-improving loops. This transition involves shifting from a reliance on human conduits for information to a system where the entirety of a company’s domain knowledge is made legible to AI. The core operational philosophy moves from scaling headcount to burning tokens, where systems autonomously identify failures, write code to fix them, and deploy updates overnight. In this new paradigm, middle management is eliminated in favor of individual contributors and directly responsible individuals, while humans focus on high stakes, emotional, and novel situations at the edge of the company brain.

## **The Shift from Hierarchical to Intelligent Organizations**

Current corporate structures are largely designed to project power and manage information flow through nested hierarchies with consistent spans of control, much like a Roman Legion. In this model, humans serve as the primary conduits for passing orders down and sending information up the hierarchy. This structure assumes that economic value must be organized through these rigid human layers.

The emergence of AI breaks this assumption. While early adoption focused on productivity gains, such as making engineers 20 percent more effective, this approach is limited because it merely adds a more powerful engine to an old way of working. A truly AI native organization extracts domain knowledge from emails, **Slack** messages, and internal documents to create a legible intelligence layer. This allows the company to function as an intelligent organization rather than a human conduit hierarchy.

## **The Architecture of a Self-Improving Loop**

A self-improving company operates through recursive loops that function without constant human intervention. These loops consist of several distinct layers:

* **Sensor Layer:** Collects information from the outside world, such as customer emails, support tickets, code changes, and product telemetry.  
* **Policy and Decision Layer:** Defines the rules for what the system can do, what requires human permission, and what must be logged.  
* **Tool Layer:** Consists of deterministic APIs and code that the AI can call, such as querying a database or accessing a calendar.  
* **Quality Gate:** Implements evaluation checks, safety filters, and human review for high risk actions.  
* **Learning Mechanism:** Identifies where the system failed to interact correctly with the real world and feeds that information back into the top of the loop.

An example of this in practice is an agent that monitors database queries. When a query fails, a monitoring agent analyzes the failure, identifies the necessary fix, writes a merge request for the codebase, and deploys it. By the time a human returns the next day, the system has already improved itself.

## **Organizational Implications and Resource Allocation**

As companies transition to AI native models, the metrics for success and the requirements for labor change significantly.

### **From Headcount to Token Usage**

Economic efficiency is increasingly measured by revenue per employee. Companies are reaching significant milestones with five times more revenue per employee than was possible 18 months ago. Organizations should focus on burning tokens rather than increasing headcount. Monitoring token usage within a company, while potentially gameable, serves as a directional indicator of which employees are effectively leveraging AI intelligence.

### **The End of Middle Management**

The coordination problems previously solved by middle management, are now better handled by AI. The organizational structure should shift toward two primary roles:

1. **Individual Contributors (ICs):** Builders and operators who get work done directly.  
2. **Directly Responsible Individuals (DRIs):** Single, named humans responsible for specific outcomes, rather than committees or groups.

## **Achieving AI Legibility**

For a company to become AI native, the entire organization must be made legible to the Intelligence. This requires recording and digitizing every interaction.

| Category | Requirement for Legibility |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Communication** | Every email, **Slack** message, and DM must be stored in a centralized database. |
| **Oral Knowledge** | Office hours and meetings must be recorded and transcribed. |
| **Synthesis** | Raw data must be diarized and aggregated into important context for the AI. |
| **Verification** | If an event or piece of information is not recorded, it effectively did not happen for the company intelligence. |

At **Y Combinator**, this approach was used to regenerate a 150 page user manual. By synthesizing 2,000 hours of recorded office hours from the previous three months, the organization created a self-improving brain of advice that updates monthly based on new interactions.

## **Ephemeral Software and Permanent Context**

In an AI native company, the relationship between data and software changes. The business context, domain knowledge, and skills are the valuable, permanent assets. The software used to execute functions is ephemeral and disposable.

Internal operations teams can now use AI to generate on demand software and dashboards. Because AI can one-shot most internal software tasks, there is no need to maintain permanent codebases for these tools. If the underlying models improve or the requirements change, the old software should be thrown away and regenerated from the original set of instructions. Data, such as markdown files of all company emails, should never be discarded, as it forms the permanent intelligence of the company brain.

## **The Role of Humans in the AI Native Model**

In a world where a company possesses a centralized intelligence brain, humans move to the periphery. They interface with reality where the models cannot yet reach.

"The humans live, I think, around the edge."

Human involvement is prioritized for:

* **Novel Situations:** Circumstances the AI has not encountered before.  
* **Ethical Considerations:** Making complex moral or philosophical judgments.  
* **High Stakes and Emotional Moments:** Situations involving deep human emotion, such as a founder dispute, or high level sales conversations.  
* **Physical Reality:** Interfacing with the real world in places where intelligence models cannot yet go.

"It’s not like a tool you give to your engineers to make them more productive, but I think you can reimagine what a company is, as a set of recursive self-improving AI loops."

"If it was recorded, it happened to the AI."

# **YC Paper Club: Inference, Diffusion, and World Models Analysis**

## **Executive Summary**

The inaugural **YC** Paper Club brought together a community of researchers and founders to analyze the shifting landscape of Artificial Intelligence, specifically focusing on the transition from training centric development to inference as a primary capability. The proceedings highlighted three critical advancements: the acceleration of model inference through parallelization, the integration of world models into robotic control, and the development of new scaling laws for data constrained environments.

Key takeaways include:

* Inference speed is increasingly viewed as a measure of peak intelligence rather than just a cost or convenience factor.  
* New algorithms such as Speculative Speculative Decoding (SSD) achieve significant speedups, reaching 300 tokens per second on large models like Llama 3 70B.  
* World models are evolving to solve the problem of representation collapse using elegant regularization techniques, such as the Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg).  
* In environments where internet data is limited, aggressive regularization (up to 30 times standard levels) and ensembling can provide up to a 5x data efficiency win.  
* Theoretical analysis of deep learning using PAC-Bayes frameworks suggests that overparameterization improves generalization because larger models find more compressible solutions and have a higher volume of flat minima.

## **Analysis of Inference and Speculative Decoding**

The traditional mental model of inference as a simple matrix multiplication process is being replaced by a sophisticated systems and algorithms approach. A central theme is the transition of inference from a cost lever to a capability lever.

### **Speculative Speculative Decoding (SSD)**

Speculative decoding typically involves a small model (the draft) generating token guesses that a larger target model verifies. The primary bottleneck in vanilla speculative decoding is the sequential dependence between the draft and the verifier. SSD addresses this by parallelizing the operations.

| Feature | Vanilla Speculative Decoding | Speculative Speculative Decoding (SSD) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Execution** | Sequential (Draft then Verify) | Parallel (Drafting while Verifying) |
| **Logic** | Draft round t+1 depends on verification of round t | Draft round t+1 anticipates outcomes of round t |
| **Latency** | High due to logical dependencies | Low due to hidden drafting latency |
| **Prediction** | Linear token generation | Predicts verification outcomes and bonus tokens |

SSD leverages the internal information of the draft model to predict what the target model is likely to accept. It achieves a 80 to 90 percent accuracy rate in predicting verification outcomes, allowing the system to hide the latency of drafting entirely. This approach is particularly effective for high throughput and low latency requirements, such as real-time code generation.

"The speed at which you can do inference, the tokens per second, is exactly the peak intelligence that you can deliver."

## **World Models and Robotics Control**

The research presented by representatives from **Google DeepMind** and **YC** founders explores how agents can learn the dynamics of their environment to perform complex tasks.

### **Diffusion Model Predictive Control (DMPC)**

DMPC uses diffusion models to handle both multistep action proposals and multistep dynamics. This factorization allows for several advantages over joint modeling:

* **Compounding Error Reduction:** Multistep modeling prevents the accumulation of errors over long horizons.  
* **Runtime Adaptation:** Because the action proposal and dynamics are separate, the model can adapt to novel rewards (e.g., changing a task from running to jumping) or novel dynamics (e.g., a robot with a broken limb) without retraining the entire system.  
* **Planning Efficiency:** The strength of diffusion modeling allows for simpler, sampling based planners to outperform previous complex approaches.

### **Latent World Models (LWM)**

Training world models involves the risk of representation collapse, where the model essentially does nothing because the optimization finds a trivial local minimum. The LWM approach uses Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) and a specific regularizer to ensure healthy latent spaces.

The SIGReg regularizer (Sketched Isotropic Gaussian) ensures:

1. **Sketching:** One dimensional passes are made over high dimensional data.  
2. **Isotropic:** The distribution looks the same when sliced from any direction.  
3. **Gaussian:** Each slice of the latent embedding distribution is Gaussian.

This method is approximately 50 times faster than competing world models because it operates entirely within the latent space and requires only 15 million parameters. Furthermore, LWM enables uncertainty quantification, where a spike in model error can signal a perturbation in the environment, a feature not natively available in model-free approaches.

## **Theoretical Generalization and Scaling Laws**

As the industry faces a potential data plateau, researchers are revisiting classical machine learning theories to understand why modern models generalize and how to optimize them when data is scarce.

### **Dispelling the Mystery of Overparameterization**

Classical PAC-Bayes theory is used to explain why larger models do not necessarily overfit. Research conducted by **Q Labs** and their collaborators indicates:

* **Compressibility:** As model parameter size increases, researchers find more efficient encodings of the training set. There is a negative correlation between the bits required to encode a training set and the number of parameters.  
* **Flat Minima:** Overparameterization increases the volume of flat minima in parameter space exponentially compared to sharp minima. Flat minima are more compressible and lead to better generalization.  
* **Soft Inductive Bias:** Models must have an inductive bias to generalize, and deep learning models act as expressive hypothesis spaces with a bias toward solutions that are more compressible.

### **Data Constrained Scaling**

Projections suggest human-generated internet data grows by 3 percent annually, while compute spend grows by 4 to 5 times. This shift creates a regime where compute is infinite but data is constrained.

"The amount of compute that we're willing to spend per data point, is going to continue to increase by roughly 4x year over year."

A joint scaling recipe was proposed to maximize performance under these constraints:

1. **Aggressive Regularization:** Using weight decay rates 30 times higher than compute optimal standards allows models to continue learning without immediate overfitting.  
2. **Ensembling:** Training multiple models and ensembling them is more data efficient than training a single large model of the same total parameter count.  
3. **Distillation:** The gains from large ensembles can be distilled into smaller, dense models, retaining approximately 83 percent of the loss improvement. This allows the benefits of data efficiency to be realized at a lower inference cost.  
4. **Self-Distillation:** Distilling a model into a fresh version of itself can unexpectedly improve loss, even beating the performance of heavily regularized models.

This joint recipe offers a 5x data efficiency win, which remains constant even when scaled to trillions of tokens. Experiments in continued pre-training showed that these techniques could match the performance of a model trained on 73 billion tokens using only 4 billion tokens, representing a 17x win in specific math-related contexts.
