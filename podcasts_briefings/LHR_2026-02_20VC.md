# **Demis Hassabis on AGI Development and Global Impacts**

## **Executive Summary**

The transition toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), represents a technological shift comparable to ten times the magnitude of the Industrial Revolution occurring at ten times the speed. Current projections from leadership at **Google DeepMind** indicate a high probability of achieving AGI within the next five years, aligning with long term forecasts established in 2010\. While compute remains the primary bottleneck for both scaling and experimentation, the industry is entering a phase where algorithmic innovation will provide a greater competitive advantage than raw scaling alone.

The most significant immediate benefits are expected in scientific discovery and medicine, specifically through the acceleration of drug design and the optimization of national energy grids. However, this progress necessitates rigorous international safety standards and a proactive approach to labor displacement and wealth redistribution. Strategic focus is shifting from simple foundation models to integrated systems capable of planning, memory, and continual learning.

## **The Path to Artificial General Intelligence**

### **Definition and Timing**

AGI is defined as a system that exhibits all the cognitive capabilities of the human mind. The human brain serves as the only existing proof that general intelligence is possible in the universe. Current assessments suggest that the industry is on track for a twenty year development arc that began in 2010\.

* **Timeline:** There is a strong possibility of achieving AGI by 2029 or 2030\.  
* **Historical Accuracy:** Predictions made by **Google DeepMind** co-founders in 2010 regarding compute and algorithmic progress have remained consistent and accurate over the last fourteen years.

### **Scaling and Diminishing Returns**

While scaling laws continue to provide substantial returns, the exponential jumps in performance seen in early generations of large language models are beginning to transition into a more nuanced phase.

| Aspect of Scaling | Current Status |
| :---- | :---- |
| Performance Gains | Gains remain substantial but are less than the initial doubling seen in early generations. |
| Competitive Advantage | Leading labs are pulling away from others as AI tools are used to build the next generation of models. |
| Algorithmic Value | Labs capable of inventing new algorithmic ideas will hold a significant advantage as existing ideas are exhausted. |

## **Technical Frontiers and Bottlenecks**

### **Primary Resource Constraints**

Compute is identified as the most significant bottleneck. Beyond the obvious need for larger architectures and more parameters, compute functions as the workbench for researchers. New algorithmic ideas must be tested at a reasonable scale to ensure they remain viable when integrated into main systems.

### **Missing Cognitive Capabilities**

Despite rapid progress in video models and interactive world models, several critical capabilities are currently absent from frontier AI systems.

* **Continual Learning:** Systems do not currently learn after their initial training phase is complete. There is a need for mechanisms similar to biological sleep or consolidation to incorporate new information into existing knowledge bases.  
* **Memory Systems:** Current long context windows are viewed as a brute force approach. More sophisticated architectures for memory are required.  
* **Hierarchical Planning:** Existing systems struggle with long term planning horizons that span several years.  
* **Consistency:** AI currently exhibits jagged intelligence, where it performs exceptionally on specific tasks but fails at elementary problems when they are framed differently.

## **Transformative Impacts on Science and Medicine**

### **Drug Discovery and Isomorphic Labs**

The application of AI to science is a primary driver for AGI development. **Isomorphic Labs**, a spinout from **Google DeepMind**, is working to solve the entire drug discovery process, focusing on chemistry, compound design, and toxicity prediction.

"I think we will have that whole drug design engine ready in, you know the next 5, plus 5 to 10 years, then you are right."

### **Clinical Trials and Regulation**

The next challenge involves the length of clinical trials, which often take a decade. AI can mitigate these delays by:

1. Simulating parts of the human metabolism.  
2. Stratifying patients based on genomic makeup to ensure the right drug reaches the right candidate.  
3. Backtesting model predictions against real world data to eventually allow regulators to skip certain steps, such as animal testing.

## **AI Safety and Global Governance**

### **Safety Risks**

There are two primary concerns regarding safety as systems become more autonomous and agentic. The first is the misuse of dual purpose technology by bad actors for harmful ends. The second is a technical challenge involving alignment and ensuring systems stay within intended guardrails.

### **Regulatory Proposals**

The current international system is fragmented, which is not ideal for the most consequential technology in history. A proposed framework for safety includes:

* **International Standards:** An international body similar to the atomic energy agency should be established.  
* **Minimum Benchmarks:** Testing for undesirable traits such as deception.  
* **Technical Safeguards:** Preventing systems from outputting tokens in machine languages that are not human readable to avoid hidden vulnerabilities.  
* **Certification:** A kite mark of quality to guarantee that models meet specific safety standards.  
* **AI Safety Institutes:** Implementation of technical auditing bodies, similar to those in the UK and USA, staffed with high quality researchers.

## **Socio-Economic Disruptions**

### **Labor Market Shifts**

Historically, revolutionary technologies have disrupted jobs while creating new, higher paying roles. However, the speed of AGI may create unprecedented upheaval.

"I sometimes quantify the coming of AGI as 10 times the industrial revolution at 10 times the speed."

### **Wealth and Energy**

To combat income inequality and the concentration of wealth, strategies such as sovereign wealth funds or pension fund investments in major AI companies are suggested. Additionally, AI is expected to resolve the energy crisis it contributes to by:

* Increasing national grid efficiency by 30 to 40 percent.  
* Accelerating breakthroughs in fusion energy through partnerships with firms like **Commonwealth Fusion**.  
* Advancing material science for better batteries and superconductors.

## **Strategic Geography and Industry Structure**

### **The UK and European Ecosystem**

London is positioned as a critical hub for deep tech due to its proximity to world-class universities like **Cambridge**, **Oxford**, **Imperial College**, and **UCL**. Staying outside of Silicon Valley is seen as an advantage, as it allows for more original, deep thinking away from industry fads.

### **Barriers to Growth**

While Europe and the UK excel at the startup phase, they face a chasm when attempting to build trillion dollar companies. The primary obstacles are:

1. **Fragmented Markets:** The need for a more unified European market for innovation.  
2. **Capital Access:** A lack of billion dollar funding rounds to compete with incumbents.  
3. **Investment Regulations:** The need to unlock pension fund capital for growth stage technology.

"I think unlocking what pension funds can invest in or just for the kind of growth stage. I think we are brilliant at doing the startup idea, and getting it to a certain level like we did with DeepMind."

# **Anjney Midha on the Evolution of Frontier AI Systems, Infrastructure, and Capital**

## **Executive Summary**

This document synthesizes the strategic insights and analysis provided by [Anjney Midha](https://www.linkedin.com/in/anjney), the founder of **AMP** and a founding investor in **Anthropic**. The document examines the shifting landscape of Artificial Intelligence, focusing on the systemic bottlenecks to progress, the requirement for sovereign infrastructure, and the transition toward a new era of venture capital and public benefit governance.

The progression of frontier AI is currently constrained by four primary bottlenecks: culture, context, compute, and capital. While scaling laws continue to hold, specifically in domains like material science and coding, the industry has entered a pre-standardization era analogous to the early days of the electricity grid in 1885\. A critical transition is underway from viewing AI as a foundation model business to understanding it as a frontier systems business. Strategic success now requires the coordination of massive capital outlays, approximately $40 billion for a 1.3 gigawatt grid in one instance, to secure non fungible compute resources. Furthermore, the rise of adversarial distillation and the implications of the Cloud Act necessitate the development of a Western Iron Dome for inference and the establishment of sovereign AI stacks in regions like Europe to ensure long term independence and security.

## **The Four Bottlenecks to Frontier AI Progress**

The current phase of AI development is defined by specific limitations that determine the rate of capability advancement.

* **Culture:** [Midha](https://www.linkedin.com/in/anjney) identifies culture as the most significant bottleneck. A mission driven culture is the primary driver for attracting top tier research talent. When the right culture is established, algorithmic innovation becomes an organic output because researchers remain flexible regarding architecture and focused on solving core problems.  
* **Context:** This represents the data required to push the frontier in specific domains. While internet data is sufficient for general models, it is inadequate for fields like material science or physics where data is locked in national labs or physical manufacturing plants. Companies must build integrated feedback loops, such as using robots to physically verify LLM predictions, to overcome this data shortage.  
* **Compute:** Compute is currently a starved and non fungible resource. The lack of standardization across different chip generations, such as **Nvidia** H100s versus GB200s, creates stranded pockets of unutilized compute, leading to an infrastructure wastage crisis.  
* **Capital:** The scale of investment required for frontier systems necessitates sophisticated financial structures. This includes a mix of equity and debt, similar to the funding of large scale utilities or industrial infrastructure.

## **The Infrastructure Crisis and the AI Grid**

The AI industry is currently in a pre-standardization era, similar to the industrial revolution in 1885 England.

### **The Problem of Non-Fungibility**

Current computation units, or flops, are not born equal. Clusters provisioned with older chips cannot easily be used for the training or inference of newer, more advanced models due to memory constraints. This lack of standardization leads to a boom and bust cycle where companies hoard resources, yet billions of dollars in compute sit unutilized because they cannot flow across secure boundaries or different chip types.

### **The ISO Model for Compute**

To address this, **AMP** is positioned as an independent system operator of a compute grid rather than a traditional cloud provider. This model aims to:

* Coordinate capacity across the independent ecosystem.  
* Allow teams to provision for base loads while providing the ability to spike capacity for training or inference.  
* Secure massive infrastructure pipelines, such as the 1.3 gigawatt project which represents roughly $40 billion in cloud spend over four years.

## **Sovereign AI and Western Security**

Geopolitical and legal factors are driving a move toward localized and secure AI infrastructure.

### **Sovereignty in Europe**

The US Cloud Act, which allows the American government to access data on infrastructure managed by American companies, creates a conflict for European mission critical workloads in defense and logistics. This has necessitated the rise of local infrastructure and models, exemplified by **Mistral** in France. The goal is a full independent stack, including land, power, compute, and open source models trained locally.

### **The Iron Dome for Inference**

Western AI labs face increasing state sponsored threats, particularly through adversarial distillation. This process involves taking Western models, distilling their state of the art capabilities through various endpoints, and using that data to improve domestic models. [Midha](https://www.linkedin.com/in/anjney) proposes a coordinated Iron Dome for inference, a shared proxy and deployment coordination protocol that would allow Western companies to detect and defend against these attacks collectively.

## **The Evolution of Venture Capital and Governance**

The funding and building of AI companies are returning to a model common in the early days of the semiconductor and biotech industries.

### **Back to the Future Venture Capital**

The era of simply writing checks to SaaS companies is being superseded by an incubation heavy model. This involves investors acting as co-founders who handle capex, supply chains, and capital structures, allowing scientists to focus on engineering. [Midha](https://www.linkedin.com/in/anjney) cites historical examples like [Arthur Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Rock) at **Intel** and the founding of **Genentech** in the basement of **Kleiner Perkins** as the blueprint for this approach.

### **Public Benefit Corporations (PBCs)**

To manage the inherent tension between mission and profit, many frontier companies, including **AMP** and **Anthropic**, utilize public benefit charters. This governance structure allows leadership to make long term decisions that prioritize humanity or ecosystem health, such as providing compute at cost, which might not be immediately legible to traditional shareholders.

Following the decline of pandemic related demand, **Curative** transitioned into the health insurance sector, currently valued at $1.3 billion. ## **The Evolution of Curative: From Cattle to COVID-19**

The history of **Curative** involves a series of technical pivots driven by market size and regulatory opportunities.

* **TL Biolabs and Early Diagnostics:** The company began in the United Kingdom as **TL Biolabs**, focusing on sequencing DNA from dairy and beef cattle to predict milk yield and musculature. Despite early success, [Turner](https://www.linkedin.com/in/fred-turner) found the UK investment environment focused on mitigating risk rather than optimizing outcomes, stating, "The UK doesn't have, like some of the the kind of infrastructure for startups and investing, of like that many people that have kind of done a startup before, and then are willing to invest in the next generation."  
* **Move to Silicon Valley:** After joining a **Y Combinator** batch and receiving seed funding from **Andreessen Horowitz**, the company moved to the United States. A realization that the total addressable market for cattle testing was capped at $1.5 billion, led to a pivot toward human diagnostics.  
* **Shield and the Sepsis Market:** The company rebranded as **Shield** to focus on antibiotic resistance in STDs and later sepsis. Sepsis was identified as a critical market due to its high mortality rate, and the failure of existing diagnostics in community hospitals. Although a series B round fell through in late 2019, the core team and laboratory licenses remained in place as the COVID-19 pandemic began.

## **Scaling During the Pandemic**

**Curative** underwent a massive operational expansion to meet the demand for COVID-19 testing, utilizing non-traditional methods to bypass supply chain bottlenecks.

* **Orthogonal Supply Chains:** To avoid competition for standard medical supplies, **Curative** sourced alternative materials, such as electronic testing swabs and filter plates for extraction. [Turner](https://www.linkedin.com/in/fred-turner) described this as "We built what we called an orthogonal supply chain, which is just basically a fancy way of saying, we don't use the things other people use."  
* **Rapid Growth Metrics:**  
  * **Revenue:** Approximately $5 billion over a three year period.  
  * **Headcount:** Expanded from 7 to 7,000 employees in nine months.  
  * **Testing Volume:** Peaked at 206,000 tests in a single day in December 2020\.  
  * **Vaccinations:** Administered 2.5 million doses, though this was a loss making endeavor due to low government reimbursement rates.  
* **The Pivot to Insurance:** Recognizing that COVID-19 testing was a temporary market, the company invested $500 million of its profits into launching a health insurance provider.

## **AI Integration and the Agentic Workforce**

A significant portion of the current operational strategy involves using AI agents to displace traditional administrative functions and legacy software.

### **Software Replacement and Cost Reduction**

**Curative** is aggressively reducing its reliance on external software vendors.

* **Salesforce Cancellation:** The company canceled a $600,000 annual **Salesforce** contract after building an internal CRM in two months using AI assisted coding.  
* **SAS Spend:** The organization aims to cut 80% of its SAS spend this year, targeting legacy platforms with poor APIs and high maintenance costs.  
* **Internal Development:** Technical resources are being shifted from managing external platforms to building custom internal tools that integrate directly with AI agents.

### **Agentic Workflows**

The company employs specialized AI agents, such as one named Gwen, to handle complex back office tasks.

* **Contracting and Sales:** Gwen researches providers, identifies contact information via **ZoomInfo**, and conducts email negotiations. This agent increased contract volume from 100 per week to 100 per day.  
* **Legal and Credentialing:** AI agents now handle medical license verification and transcript checks. A process that previously took three months and $50 per doctor now takes 12 hours and costs $0.20.  
* **Codegen Strategy:** Instead of standardizing data formats for external partners, **Curative** uses models like those from **Anthropic** to write single-use Python scripts that parse and reformat diverse data inputs.

### **Financial Impact of AI**

The company’s utilization of **Anthropic** models has grown significantly, with costs reaching millions of dollars per month. [Turner](https://www.linkedin.com/in/fred-turner) noted that even if **Anthropic** doubled its pricing, the cost benefit ratio would remain favorable compared to human labor, stating "With people, it cost us about $1,500 to $2,000 on average to do a contract, the average with Gwen has been about $70 so it would still work fine."

## **Analysis of the United States Healthcare System**

The strategic shift into health insurance was motivated by the belief that the payer is the primary driver of behavior in the US healthcare system.

* **Consolidation Inefficiency:** The market is currently dominated by four large payers and several ultra consolidated hospital systems. This lack of competition allows hospital systems to demand higher rates for primary care by leveraging their specialized surgical centers.  
* **Regulatory Disincentives:** [Turner](https://www.linkedin.com/in/fred-turner) criticized provisions in the Affordable Care Act that cap insurance profits at 15%. This creates an incentive for insurers to increase total healthcare spending rather than reduce it, as a 15% margin on a larger total spend results in higher absolute profit.  
* **Quality of Care:** Despite systemic inefficiencies, the United States remains the premier location for cutting edge medical techniques and drugs due to high levels of investment and access.

## **Future Frontiers: Nuclear Energy and Subcritical**

In addition to healthcare, leadership has co-founded **Subcritical Systems**, a company aimed at revolutionizing nuclear fission.

* **Technology:** The company utilizes an energy amplifier approach. Unlike traditional reactors that balance on a critical threshold, this system operates at 0.97 criticality, meaning the reaction naturally fizzles out without an external particle accelerator.  
* **Safety and Regulation:** This design provides an on/off switch for fission, theoretically making runaway reactions impossible. [Turner](https://www.linkedin.com/in/fred-turner) argues that nuclear power's primary obstacles are regulatory rather than scientific, noting that the technology to build safe reactors has existed since the 1960s.  
* **Market Opportunity:** The total addressable market for nuclear energy is estimated to be in the trillions, similar in scale to the US healthcare market.
