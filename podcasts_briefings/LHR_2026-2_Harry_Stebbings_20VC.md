# **Demis Hassabis on AGI Development and Global Impacts**

## **Executive Summary**

The transition toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), represents a technological shift comparable to ten times the magnitude of the Industrial Revolution occurring at ten times the speed. Current projections from leadership at **Google DeepMind** indicate a high probability of achieving AGI within the next five years, aligning with long term forecasts established in 2010\. While compute remains the primary bottleneck for both scaling and experimentation, the industry is entering a phase where algorithmic innovation will provide a greater competitive advantage than raw scaling alone.

The most significant immediate benefits are expected in scientific discovery and medicine, specifically through the acceleration of drug design and the optimization of national energy grids. However, this progress necessitates rigorous international safety standards and a proactive approach to labor displacement and wealth redistribution. Strategic focus is shifting from simple foundation models to integrated systems capable of planning, memory, and continual learning.

## **The Path to Artificial General Intelligence**

### **Definition and Timing**

AGI is defined as a system that exhibits all the cognitive capabilities of the human mind. The human brain serves as the only existing proof that general intelligence is possible in the universe. Current assessments suggest that the industry is on track for a twenty year development arc that began in 2010\.

* **Timeline:** There is a strong possibility of achieving AGI by 2029 or 2030\.  
* **Historical Accuracy:** Predictions made by **Google DeepMind** co-founders in 2010 regarding compute and algorithmic progress have remained consistent and accurate over the last fourteen years.

### **Scaling and Diminishing Returns**

While scaling laws continue to provide substantial returns, the exponential jumps in performance seen in early generations of large language models are beginning to transition into a more nuanced phase.

| Aspect of Scaling | Current Status |
| :---- | :---- |
| Performance Gains | Gains remain substantial but are less than the initial doubling seen in early generations. |
| Competitive Advantage | Leading labs are pulling away from others as AI tools are used to build the next generation of models. |
| Algorithmic Value | Labs capable of inventing new algorithmic ideas will hold a significant advantage as existing ideas are exhausted. |

## **Technical Frontiers and Bottlenecks**

### **Primary Resource Constraints**

Compute is identified as the most significant bottleneck. Beyond the obvious need for larger architectures and more parameters, compute functions as the workbench for researchers. New algorithmic ideas must be tested at a reasonable scale to ensure they remain viable when integrated into main systems.

### **Missing Cognitive Capabilities**

Despite rapid progress in video models and interactive world models, several critical capabilities are currently absent from frontier AI systems.

* **Continual Learning:** Systems do not currently learn after their initial training phase is complete. There is a need for mechanisms similar to biological sleep or consolidation to incorporate new information into existing knowledge bases.  
* **Memory Systems:** Current long context windows are viewed as a brute force approach. More sophisticated architectures for memory are required.  
* **Hierarchical Planning:** Existing systems struggle with long term planning horizons that span several years.  
* **Consistency:** AI currently exhibits jagged intelligence, where it performs exceptionally on specific tasks but fails at elementary problems when they are framed differently.

## **Transformative Impacts on Science and Medicine**

### **Drug Discovery and Isomorphic Labs**

The application of AI to science is a primary driver for AGI development. **Isomorphic Labs**, a spinout from **Google DeepMind**, is working to solve the entire drug discovery process, focusing on chemistry, compound design, and toxicity prediction.

"I think we will have that whole drug design engine ready in, you know the next 5, plus 5 to 10 years, then you are right."

### **Clinical Trials and Regulation**

The next challenge involves the length of clinical trials, which often take a decade. AI can mitigate these delays by:

1. Simulating parts of the human metabolism.  
2. Stratifying patients based on genomic makeup to ensure the right drug reaches the right candidate.  
3. Backtesting model predictions against real world data to eventually allow regulators to skip certain steps, such as animal testing.

## **AI Safety and Global Governance**

### **Safety Risks**

There are two primary concerns regarding safety as systems become more autonomous and agentic. The first is the misuse of dual purpose technology by bad actors for harmful ends. The second is a technical challenge involving alignment and ensuring systems stay within intended guardrails.

### **Regulatory Proposals**

The current international system is fragmented, which is not ideal for the most consequential technology in history. A proposed framework for safety includes:

* **International Standards:** An international body similar to the atomic energy agency should be established.  
* **Minimum Benchmarks:** Testing for undesirable traits such as deception.  
* **Technical Safeguards:** Preventing systems from outputting tokens in machine languages that are not human readable to avoid hidden vulnerabilities.  
* **Certification:** A kite mark of quality to guarantee that models meet specific safety standards.  
* **AI Safety Institutes:** Implementation of technical auditing bodies, similar to those in the UK and USA, staffed with high quality researchers.

## **Socio-Economic Disruptions**

### **Labor Market Shifts**

Historically, revolutionary technologies have disrupted jobs while creating new, higher paying roles. However, the speed of AGI may create unprecedented upheaval.

"I sometimes quantify the coming of AGI as 10 times the industrial revolution at 10 times the speed."

### **Wealth and Energy**

To combat income inequality and the concentration of wealth, strategies such as sovereign wealth funds or pension fund investments in major AI companies are suggested. Additionally, AI is expected to resolve the energy crisis it contributes to by:

* Increasing national grid efficiency by 30 to 40 percent.  
* Accelerating breakthroughs in fusion energy through partnerships with firms like **Commonwealth Fusion**.  
* Advancing material science for better batteries and superconductors.

## **Strategic Geography and Industry Structure**

### **The UK and European Ecosystem**

London is positioned as a critical hub for deep tech due to its proximity to world-class universities like **Cambridge**, **Oxford**, **Imperial College**, and **UCL**. Staying outside of Silicon Valley is seen as an advantage, as it allows for more original, deep thinking away from industry fads.

### **Barriers to Growth**

While Europe and the UK excel at the startup phase, they face a chasm when attempting to build trillion dollar companies. The primary obstacles are:

1. **Fragmented Markets:** The need for a more unified European market for innovation.  
2. **Capital Access:** A lack of billion dollar funding rounds to compete with incumbents.  
3. **Investment Regulations:** The need to unlock pension fund capital for growth stage technology.

"I think unlocking what pension funds can invest in or just for the kind of growth stage. I think we are brilliant at doing the startup idea, and getting it to a certain level like we did with DeepMind."

# **AI Market Dynamics and Private Company Valuations**

## **Executive Summary**

The current technological landscape is defined by an unprecedented concentration of value in three primary entities, **SpaceX**, **OpenAI**, and **Anthropic**, whose combined valuations at the time of their eventual initial public offerings are projected to exceed all other such offerings from the past twenty five years combined. **Anthropic** has demonstrated extraordinary growth, reaching a 30 billion dollar revenue run rate in five years, a milestone that took **Salesforce** twenty five years to achieve. This growth is accompanied by training costs that are significantly lower than those of **OpenAI**, suggesting a more efficient cost structure.

Conversely, **OpenAI** is undergoing a period of internal volatility characterized by a wholesale change in its management team and the controversial acquisition of the media asset **TBPN**. Financial analysis of **OpenAI** reveals that its recent funding round was composed largely of tranches, compute offsets from **Nvidia**, and future AGI performance targets from **Amazon**, rather than immediate cash. In the infrastructure sector, **Supabase** and **OpenRouter** are emerging as critical utilities, with **Supabase** benefiting from the trend of AI agents creating more databases than humans. Security remains a critical vulnerability, as evidenced by the **Mercor** data breach, which underscores the risk AI agents pose to established B2B firms.

## **Competitive Comparison of Anthropic and OpenAI**

The competitive gap between the two leading artificial intelligence firms has shifted, with **Anthropic** accelerating while **OpenAI** faces operational challenges.

* **Growth and Revenue Efficiency:** **Anthropic** has grown its revenue from 9 billion dollars at the start of the year to a 30 billion dollar run rate. "Anthropic got there in five, but maybe they really got there in three depending on how you count, but you just uh you know, it was it was incredible to see where they were uh, in February we couldn't believe it and then essentially adding 10 million of net AR, let's let's not debate whether it's how many RS there are, and whether it's recurring at this level of growth, it really doesn't matter, it doesn't matter so uh, and that there's still capacity constraints, and that Claude still shows us when we're in there, that it can't finish chats."  
* **Cost Structure Disparities:** Financial leaks suggest that **Anthropic** training costs are approximately one quarter of the costs incurred by **OpenAI**. This is attributed to a narrower focus that excludes video, images, and various consumer products.  
* **Compute Constraints and Monetization:** Both firms are managing capacity by allocating compute based on revenue potential. **Anthropic** has restricted its base plans to limit usage by agentic tools that consume vast amounts of tokens without generating proportional revenue. Similarly, **OpenAI** has reportedly de-emphasized video features due to high compute requirements and low revenue generation.

## **Operational Volatility at OpenAI**

**OpenAI** is currently managing a significant transition in its leadership and corporate strategy, which has sparked debate regarding its focus and stability.

* **Management Turnover:** Recent leadership changes at **OpenAI** include the relocation of the COO to special projects, the resignation of the CMO and CRO, and a leave of absence for the head of apps. "You don't just sit there and make no changes on the team, when your competitor uh, over the last 6 months has radically changed the competitive posture." The appointment of [Denise Holland Dresser](https://www.linkedin.com/in/denisedresser), formerly of **Slack** and **Salesforce**, to lead go to market efforts, is seen as a high risk move during a period of internal tumult.  
* **The TBPN Acquisition:** The purchase of the media asset **TBPN** has been characterized as a vanity project that contradicts the firm's stated focus on core AI development. While some argue it allows the company to turn its balance sheet into a marketing asset, critics suggest that **OpenAI** already commands sufficient global attention. "Owning a media asset invariably takes way more time than you think for way less money than you expect see [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos) for details."  
* **Funding Realities:** Much of **OpenAI**'s 13 billion dollar round is comprised of non cash assets. **Softbank** investments come in tranches, while **Nvidia** provides compute offsets. **Amazon**'s investment is partially contingent on the achievement of AGI or an IPO.

## **The Scaling of the Big Three and Public Market Implications**

The concentration of wealth in **SpaceX**, **OpenAI**, and **Anthropic** represents a fundamental shift in the venture capital ecosystem.

* **SpaceX IPO Prospects:** **SpaceX** has filed for a confidential IPO targeting a 2 trillion dollar valuation. The company reportedly generates 15 to 16 billion dollars in revenue with 8 billion dollars in EBITDA. The valuation is heavily influenced by the [Elon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk) premium, a sentiment that has recently declined in relation to **Tesla**.  
* **Market Concentration:** The combined value of these three private companies dwarfs the public market outcomes of the last quarter century. This concentration mirrors public markets, where a small number of firms, including **Nvidia**, **Apple**, **Microsoft**, **Alphabet**, and **Meta**, represent roughly 30 percent of the total S\&P.  
* **Venture Capital Strategy:** Firms like **Sequoia** are bringing back veteran leadership, such as [Doug Leone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Leone), to provide gravitas and continuity for limited partners during this period of rapid change and intense competition from rivals like **Founders Fund**.

## **Infrastructure, Compliance, and Security**

New leaders are emerging in the plumbing of the AI era, though they face challenges regarding security and ethical conduct.

* **Agentic Database Growth:** **Supabase** is positioned as a leader in the database sector because it facilitates the automatic deployment of databases required by AI agents. "Now, I don't have the exact numbers, but I know more databases are being created by agents and humans so that is the trend you're betting on, all right, databases are a fundamental category of software, it always has been."  
* **Marketplaces and Take Rates:** **OpenRouter** serves as an interface for over 50 different large language models, allowing developers to switch between providers dynamically. Despite growing from 10 million to 50 million in annual recurring revenue in less than a year, its 5 percent take rate raises questions about its long term ability to reach a billion dollar revenue target without diversifying its product line.  
* **Corporate Ethics at Delve:** The compliance firm **Delve** was recently expelled from the **Y Combinator** community. This followed allegations that the firm used AI to fake audits and stole intellectual property from other **Y Combinator** portfolio companies. "You broke the code of the West, you're out and I think from an enforcement perspective, you know I can totally see why they did it."  
* **Cybersecurity Threats:** The breach at **Mercor** highlights a new class of threat where AI agents automate attacks, phishing, and voice duplication at scale. "I genuinely think that most B2B companies are going to get hit worse than **Mercor**, okay this light, LLM was was one of the weaknesses that they had like tons of B2B folks have, how strong how state of the art and strong are their security teams they're, they're not they're relying on a hodgepodge of open source and other products that are barely monitored in many cases."

## **The Future of AI Driven Marketing**

The emergence of hyper personalized marketing at scale is expected to disrupt traditional advertising models within the next two years.

* **Case Study of MEDVi:** The firm **MEDVi** reportedly reached 1.8 billion dollars in revenue with a very small team by utilizing AI for mass personalization in the marketing of GLP 1 medications. While its tactics exist on the edge of regulation, the ability to use AI for high volume, targeted outreach is seen as the future of the industry.  
* **Evolution of Tactics:** Just as search engine optimization was once a dark art used primarily by lead generation firms before being adopted by **DigitalOcean** and **Zapier**, agentic marketing is expected to become a standard corporate tool. "The way we're doing advertising and marketing, will seem incredibly dated in two years, like only the creakiest companies will be doing marketing and advertising the way they do it today in two years, it makes no sense, it makes no sense." Companies that fail to leverage mass personalization are predicted to fall behind competitors who can target individual consumer needs with high precision.

# **ElevenLabs: Scaling Revenue and AI Sales Machines**

## **Executive Summary**

The following document details the sales and growth strategies of **ElevenLabs**, a rapidly scaling AI company that has evolved the traditional role of the Chief Revenue Officer (CRO). The core of the approach centers on building an AI assisted sales machine that prioritizes human-like interactions, extreme talent density, and aggressive performance targets. Central to this strategy is a 20x sales quota, a departure from the 6x to 8x industry standard, and a heavy investment in internal AI agents for customer success, proposals, and inbound lead management. The document outlines a shift from sequential market expansion to parallelization, the utilization of corporate venture capital for distribution, and a belief that the modern sales organization must operate with a smaller, highly compensated team supported by agentic systems to achieve a 50% productivity improvement.

## **The Evolution of the Chief Revenue Officer**

The role of the Chief Revenue Officer (CRO) has shifted from focusing on current transactions to architecting the distribution systems of the future. The market has evolved to allow for quicker deals, requiring a strategic focus on how AI is embedded throughout the distribution component to do more with less.

* **Strategic Focus:** The role of a CRO is fundamentally thinking about not the revenues today but the revenues of tomorrow.  
* **Operational Philosophy:** The objective is to perform difficult tasks that others are avoiding, rather than following a path of least resistance.  
* **Efficiency Goals:** **ElevenLabs** aims for a 50% improvement in productivity through AI, allowing the company to hire fewer people but compensate top talent at a higher level.

## **AI Integration in Sales and Revenue**

The current market for generic AI sales tools is viewed as ineffective because many tools treat interactions as purely transactional. Success in AI driven sales requires human-like personalization.

### **AI Agent Use Cases**

**ElevenLabs** utilizes several internal AI agents to streamline the revenue org:

| Agent Type | Function |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Inbound AI SDR** | Handles inbound leads with high success rates. |
| **AI Proposals Manager** | Scans the web for RFPs and RFIs, then scores and proposes responses. |
| **AI Customer Success Manager** | Resides in email threads, analyzes customer data, pricing, and contracts, then proactively drafts emails for human review. |

### **The Limitations of Automated Outbound**

Outbound response rates have dropped to less than 0.01% because prospects can perceive when a message is generated by AI or is purely transactional. The document asserts that, outbound is dead unless you do it with humans, or unless you do it humanly.

## **High-Performance Quotas and Compensation**

**ElevenLabs** employs a 20x sales quota, which is significantly higher than competitors such as **Clay**, who reportedly use a 6x to 8x quota.

* **Quota Philosophy:** High quotas are intended to challenge top performers. If quotas are missed because they are genuinely too high, the company adjusts them or compensates correctly, but high bars prevent complacency.  
* **Commission Structure:**  
  * Standard commission starts at 5% for anything sold.  
  * Accelerators are applied after hitting the quota (1.1x, 1.2x, 1.5x, 2x, etc.).  
  * For every extra year on a contract, reps may receive an additional 25% commission.  
  * Strategic accounts may unlock commissions for two years.  
* **Pilot Policy:** No commissions are paid on pilots because they do not contribute to the company valuation. Commissions are only paid once an annual or multi-year contract is signed.  
* **Valuation Impact:** Every $1 million in revenue is estimated to add $33 million in valuation to the company.

## **Market Expansion and Distribution Strategies**

The traditional venture capital advice of opening one market at a time is considered obsolete in the AI era due to the speed of competition.

### **Parallel Expansion**

Companies must parallelize their efforts and test multiple markets simultaneously. Go-to-market is viewed similarly to venture capital investing, where one must test a hundred things to find five or six that perform well.

### **Talent Acquisition**

There is a strategic advantage in hiring sales veterans with 20 to 25 years of experience, contrary to the typical startup preference for younger talent.

* **Seniority Benefits:** Experienced reps often have existing relationships with C-level stakeholders at major firms like **HSBC** or **Barclays**, which can drastically shorten sales cycles.  
* **Cultural Fit:** High expectations are set upfront. New hires are warned that the environment is difficult and requires full commitment.  
* **Headcount Strategy:** The sales team currently consists of 130 people, with plans to add 120 more, keeping the total under 250 while aiming for revenue growth that outpaces headcount growth.

### **India Case Study**

A mistake was made in India by verticalizing and segmenting the sales team too early. This depressed revenue for a quarter. The solution was to return to a horizontal structure to build liquidity and confidence in the pipeline before attempting to segment again.

## **Customer Success and Retention**

In the AI sector, customer success cannot be a mere satisfaction function because competitors can replicate products quickly.

* **Revenue Generation:** The document states, customer success needs to be a money generation function for the business.  
* **Deep Integration:** CSMs must go as deep as possible with customers early on to ensure retention and facilitate expansion.  
* **Community and Trust:** Building a community is essential for long term retention, distinguishing the company from a purely transactional services business.

## **Partnerships and Corporate Venture Capital (CVC)**

Strategic partnerships, particularly with companies that have a CVC arm, are a core distribution strategy.

* **Incentive Alignment:** **ElevenLabs** has partnered with **Woven Capital** (**Toyota**), **Deutsche Telekom**, **Telefonica**, and **Liberty Global**.  
* **Allocation for Revenue:** Investment allocation is often tied to revenue promises. For every million dollars invested, the partner may be expected to bring in a specific amount of revenue over 12 to 24 months.  
* **Industry Insights:** These partners provide deep domain expertise in sectors like telecommunications or automotive that the startup would not otherwise possess.

## **Marketing and ROI Analysis**

Marketing activities are judged strictly by their return on investment (ROI) and their ability to create FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out).

* **High ROI Activities:** Executive dinners (15 people) are cited as the most effective marketing tool. They facilitate competitor networking and create organic FOMO.  
* **Low ROI Activities:** Trade shows and large conferences are generally considered to have poor ROI.  
* **Owned Events:** The company prefers running its own high production summits, which focus on featuring partners and customers rather than being purely sales driven.

## **Foundational Models and Competition**

The document identifies **OpenAI**, **Anthropic**, and **Cursor** as the current blue chip brands in the AI space.

* **Open Source vs. Proprietary:** While open source models may match proprietary quality, the operational burden of scaling and maintaining them often leads enterprises back to managed services like **ElevenLabs**.  
* **Market Positioning:** **ElevenLabs** views itself as an infrastructure layer. It is willing to power competitors because the market is large enough for multiple entities to coexist. The company has a policy of transparency, notifying partners when it intends to launch a competing product.  
* **Future Outlook:** There is an expectation of a new wave of foundational model companies, though established players like **OpenAI**, **Anthropic**, **Google**, and **ElevenLabs** may eventually acquire them.
