# **The New Paradigm of AI Investing and Business Models**

## **Executive Summary**

The current venture capital ecosystem is experiencing a state of disorientation as the traditional rules of software investing are upended by the AI paradigm. Historically, software companies followed a linear path of derisking through product-market fit, unit economics, and total addressable market expansion, where increased scale equated to decreased risk. In the AI era, this relationship has flattened or even reversed, as companies exceeding one billion dollars in revenue often remain without proven unit economics or durable product differentiation.

The transition from spreadsheet based investing to an era of capital intensive AI development has introduced several critical shifts:

* **The Death of Traditional Metrics:** Classic benchmarks such as the rule of 40, high gross margins, and capital light operations are no longer reliable indicators of quality, high gross margins in AI may actually indicate a lack of feature usage.  
* **Monetization of Work:** Revenue models are shifting from seat based licensing to the monetization of inference and intelligence on tap, representing a move toward selling actual work rather than software access.  
* **Market Liquidity Shock:** Massive private funding rounds, including a hypothetical scenario where **Anthropic** could reach a 1.5 trillion dollar valuation, suggest a future liquidity event that will fundamentally alter the Silicon Valley ecosystem.  
* **Strategic Flexibility:** Firms like **Benchmark** maintain a founder-out strategy rather than a theme-in approach, prioritizing exceptional entrepreneurs who can navigate the jagged capabilities of frontier and open source models.

## **The Disorientation of the Venture Ecosystem**

The venture growth ecosystem is currently navigating a phase where previous golden rules of investing no longer apply. Historically, software startups sequentially derisked their business as they scaled. This inverse relationship between scale and risk meant that the larger a company became, the less likely it was to face a total impairment of capital.

"In the new AI paradigm, you can have businesses that are well over a billion dollars in revenue, that haven't proven out their unit economics, they haven't proven out durable product differentiation."

The current market environment suggests that risk may remain flat or even correlate positively with scale. This disorientation stems from several departures from the traditional software as a service (SaaS) model:

* **Capital Intensity:** Unlike the capital light era of software, AI requires significant capital outlays for GPUs and research labs.  
* **Gross Margin Compression:** Pure software once commanded 70% to 90% gross margins. AI inference costs naturally depress these margins, creating a bizarro world where high margins might signal that customers are not utilizing expensive AI features.  
* **Implementation Load:** The rise of field deployed engineers (FDEs) as a distribution strategy, described as the **Palantir**\-ification of the market, has replaced product-led growth (PLG) in many high scale AI companies.

## **Economic Structures: SaaS vs. AI Taxonomy**

The transition from SaaS to AI requires a new understanding of company quality. While SaaS companies were legible via spreadsheets and matured into capital light, high free cash flow businesses, AI companies exhibit diverse and often contradictory economic profiles.

### **Comparison of AI Business Models: Crusoe vs. Fireworks**

| Feature | Crusoe | Fireworks |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Primary Business** | Data center construction and infrastructure | AI inference cloud platform |
| **Capital Intensity** | High (Acquiring power, land, and permits) | Lower (Leases capacity and GPUs from partners) |
| **Value Add** | Physical infrastructure and power management | Software optimization for latency and cost |
| **Economic Model** | Serves hyperscalers and provides cloud products | Monetizes inference and software efficiency |

### **The P*Q*M Framework**

Investors are rethinking the Econ 101 variables of price (P), quantity (Q), and margin (M) for the AI era:

* **Price (P):** Annual contract values (ACV) are significantly higher. AI companies are securing nine figure contracts with startups, a rarity in traditional SaaS.  
* **Quantity (Q):** The target market remains similar to SaaS, focusing on broad enterprise adoption.  
* **Margin (M):** Margins are definitively lower, often falling below the 70% benchmark for nearly 99% of AI application companies.

## **The Inference Waterfall and the Agent Economy**

The monetization of inference, is described as a waterfall of revenue that is enabling unprecedented growth rates. Companies are no longer restricted by head count based pricing but are instead capturing value through usage based or outcome based models.

"The waterfall is inference, and just the absolute unbelievable wave of revenue and demand for so many different businesses and business models that has come out of inference."

### **The Rise of AI Agents**

The shift toward agents represents both a product and business model innovation. It allows buyers to move their mental framing from purchasing a license to purchasing intelligence or white collar activity on tap via API.

* **Coding Agents:** In the case of **Anthropic**'s Claude Code, individual developers are spending approximately 3,000 dollars per month, leading to 36,000 dollars per developer annually.  
* **Outcome-based Pricing:** Companies like **Sierra** utilize pricing based on completed deflections of customer support queries rather than raw software access.  
* **Gumloop:** This platform provides a collaborative AI agent and automation canvas for enterprises, allowing non technical employees to automate redundant work.

## **Model Utility and Frontier Intelligence**

The market is characterized by a mix of frontier models and specialized open source alternatives. While there is a high premium for frontier intelligence, a significant portion of tasks do not require the most advanced models.

### **The Mom Test for AI**

This test evaluates what percentage of a user's queries require frontier intelligence versus cost effective open source models. For many users, 100% of tasks can be handled by non frontier models. As models improve, a growing proportion of enterprise queries will move away from the frontier to save up to 95% on inference costs.

### **Independent Orchestration**

The jagged nature of model capabilities necessitates third party independent vendors like **Gumloop** to act as routers. Different models excel at different tasks:

* **Gemini** is recognized for multimodal capabilities.  
* **Anthropic**'s **Claude** is often cited as having superior coding models.  
* **OpenAI** remains a dominant force in general productivity, through products combining Codex and Chat.

## **Market Evolution and Strategic Investment**

The venture capital industry is evolving into a broader alternative asset management class. Firms like **General Catalyst** and **Andreessen Horowitz** now offer a wide array of products including debt, wealth management, and health insurance, whereas **Benchmark** remains focused on early stage partnership with entrepreneurs.

### **The Impact of Massive Liquidity**

The scale of current AI funding rounds is unprecedented. "When you think about the **Anthropic** $380 billion round, if **Anthropic** was to go public and get liquid at a $1.5 trillion valuation... it would return 35 times that of the **Snowflake** pre-IPO round." Such a liquidity event would create a massive shock to the ecosystem, impacting everything from the San Francisco housing market to the volume of new startup formations.

### **Benchmark's AI Portfolio Strategy**

**Benchmark** prioritizes a founder-out approach, avoiding thematic or category driven investing. Their diverse AI portfolio includes:

* **Starcloud:** Orbital data centers.  
* **Cerebras:** Hardware and public market AI play.  
* **Sierra** and **Legora:** Horizontal and vertical AI applications.  
* **LangChain** and **Fireworks:** Developer and infrastructure tools.  
* **HeyGen:** Consumer facing AI.  
* **Gumloop:** Collaborative automation.  
* **Mercor** and **Vanta:** Specialized data and security infrastructure.

The firm's success is attributed to backing entrepreneurs who can pioneer massive categories, often through pivots, such as when **Benchmark** invested in **Starcloud** just before market interest in orbital data centers accelerated.
