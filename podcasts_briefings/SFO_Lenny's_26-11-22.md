## **Summary**

Product Management is undergoing a structural shift in the AI era. While initial industry narratives suggested that automated capabilities would eliminate Product Managers by turning all team members into Builders, organizational experiments at **Atlassian** and tech job market data demonstrate that AI drastically lowers software throughput costs, shifting the operational bottleneck from output volume to strategic direction. Rather than acting as primary Decision Makers or Solution Designers, effective Product Managers drive decision velocity by actively bridging context gaps across customer, market, and business domains to build a shared brain with cross-functional teams. Organizations navigating AI transformation benefit from hiring artist archetype Product Managers who prioritize user experience (UX) and taste, alongside junior talent who adopt AI native workflows without legacy inertia, complemented by senior experts capable of identifying low quality AI outputs.

## **The Demand for Product Managers in the AI Era**

Industry assumptions early in the AI deployment cycle predicted a decline in Product Management roles as team members gained direct access to software building tools. However, market evidence demonstrates that Product Management roles are expanding faster than adjacent technical crafts. Global job market data highlights that open Engineering roles have slowed down, Design roles remain flat, and User Research roles have contracted to 108 open positions globally.

An internal experiment conducted at **Atlassian** illustrates the cause of this trend:

| Squad Configuration | Initial Result | Outcome After 1 Month | Operational Conclusion |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Removed dedicated Product Managers and Designers; converted all members into AI Builders. | Rapid initial building velocity and high output generation. | Team members naturally reverted to traditional specialized roles. | Code throughput is cheap; strategic direction remains the primary bottleneck. |

When raw output becomes low cost, organizations require more leadership focused on steering the ship rather than rowing it. Access to prototyping or coding tools does not replace domain expertise or design judgment. Just as the emergence of **Balsamiq** mockups democratized early layout design without turning Product Managers into expert Solution Designers, AI amplifies existing strengths and weaknesses equally, reinforcing the necessity of specialized skill sets.

## **Context Filling and Decision Velocity**

Product Managers do not have direct authority over squad members and do not own decision making rights. Instead, their central responsibility is driving decision velocity by building a shared brain across cross-functional teams.

When organizations scale beyond 20 to 30 people, core operational elements begin to break down, including trust, shared context, and cross-functional alignment. Product Managers resolve these points of failure by continuously closing context gaps across 3 core pillars:

* Customer context: Deep understanding of user personas, pain points, and empathy built through shared observation.  
* Product and market context: Insights into competitive dynamics, landscape shifts, and overall product vision.  
* Business context: Awareness of commercial models, financial targets, and operational realities.

Rather than authoring specifications from isolation or maintaining detailed task backlogs, effective Product Managers embed Engineers and Designers directly into customer discovery activities. For instance, involving Software Engineers directly in live customer interviews yields significantly higher empathy and innovation than passing down text summaries. When cross-functional teams share identical context, individual Engineers can make microdecisions independently, increasing total execution speed.

## **Product Manager Archetypes**

Product Management discovery styles vary based on individual strengths and naturally fall into 3 distinct archetypes within the PM Craft Triangle framework:

* Scientist: Operates through hypothesis driven discovery, quantitative experimentation, and continuous A/B testing. Common in growth focused teams.  
* General Manager: Focuses on total addressable market (TAM) analysis, commercial viability, revenue structures, and business unit performance.  
* Artist: Operates through intuition, emotional connection, and visionary user experiences (UX), often sketching target end-state solutions and working backward to define underlying problems.

While early-stage startups or zero to one initiatives require Product Managers capable of operating across all 3 modes at a basic level, larger organizations require deliberate calibration to match specific archetypes to project needs.

In the AI era, the artist archetype is trending upward in organizational demand. As base software intelligence and code generation become standardized features powered by platforms like **OpenAI** or **Anthropic**, product taste, visual flow, and overall customer experience emerge as primary competitive differentiators.

## **Integrating Junior and Senior Talent**

A common misconception among established enterprises is that AI automation eliminates the need to hire junior Product Managers. However, effective AI integration requires building complimentary teams composed of both junior and senior talent.

Research conducted by **Atlassian** indicates that 38% of junior workforce members are more likely to adopt new AI tools and alternative workflows compared to senior personnel. Junior employees enter the workforce without legacy habits to unlearn, operating naturally as AI natives. For example, younger Builders often transition directly from visual environments to vibe coding, bypassing manual syntax writing entirely.

Senior personnel remain essential due to deep domain expertise, enabling them to identify AI slop, establish evaluation criteria, and maintain quality benchmarks. Combining junior experimentation with senior domain oversight prevents organizational stagnation while maintaining output quality.

## **Solutions and AI Integration**

Standard Product Management frameworks discourage starting with solutions or bolting features onto legacy architectures. However, practical execution in scaled enterprise environments requires a more nuanced application of these concepts.

### **Starting with a Solution**

Starting with a solution is effective in 2 specific operational scenarios:

1. Paving the cow path: Providing users with an unconstrained canvas, such as general conversational chat or digital whiteboards, allows teams to observe user workarounds. Product Managers can then transform high frequency manual workarounds into structured features.  
2. Asset-based strategy: Developing product strategy by auditing existing organizational assets and technological solutions to identify new applications, similar to how **Amazon** leveraged its retail inventory infrastructure to expand into broader commerce categories.

### **Bolting On AI Capabilities**

For established enterprise products with existing user bases, attempting to rewrite core architectures from scratch creates unnecessary risk. The most effective approach to transforming users from AI novices to AI natives is integrating AI agents directly into established human workflows. For instance, adding automated agents directly to existing workflow columns inside **Atlassian** software tools enables automated processing across nearly 10 million monthly agent loop executions without disrupting established user habits.

## **Leadership Execution and Governance**

Effective leadership and organizational governance require specific operational behaviors across managerial and executive tiers:

* Dual track career paths: Strategic contribution must be decoupled from Workforce Management. Organizations should establish individual contributor tracks up to executive levels, enabling distinguished product experts to drive strategy without taking on administrative People Management.  
* Managerial engagement: New Managers often fail by going too hands-off too quickly. Effective Managers actively participate alongside their teams in reviews and exploratory problem solving rather than managing strictly from a distance.  
* Executive alignment: Founders and senior leaders often evaluate strategic decisions several steps ahead of the broader organization due to extended focus on the domain. Product Managers must fill their own context gap to understand the underlying rationale behind leadership directives before attempting to align their broader execution teams.

Enterprise software platforms supporting these scaling workflows, such as identity systems provided by **WorkOS** or Engineering productivity metrics tracked by **DX**, demonstrate that enterprise readiness requires rigorous alignment between human context, platform permissions, and automated agent actions. Enterprise scalability ultimately depends on clear context distribution across all operational layers.
